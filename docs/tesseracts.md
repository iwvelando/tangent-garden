# Four-dimensional shape studies

Choose **4D studies** in the header, or open `?study=4d`. The planar, spatial, and tesseract notebooks retain their own definitions and cameras when switching. A tesseract is a new mathematical object, not a space curve with a dummy fourth coordinate.

## Three ways of seeing

- **Shadows.** Perspective gives the familiar cube within a cube. Orthographic projection drops the fourth coordinate without perspective scaling. Six independent plane angles and a freely orbiting 3D camera expose different spatial and planar shadows.
- **Sections.** A three-dimensional hyperplane cuts the rotated tesseract. The central diagonal section is a regular octahedron; a passage from outside through the center and out again reveals the intervening polyhedra. A family of up to 25 parallel sections superimposes their xyz coordinates into a contour drawing. These contours are not translated or joined into a surface.
- **Stereographic curves.** Radially normalize the edges and parallel lines on each square face onto the unit 3-sphere, then stereographically project them. The circular arcs create a woven drawing whose lines still have precise mathematical origins. Open ends mark the boundary of the projection window, not missing connections.

The six examples range from the sparse **A cube beyond a cube** to **Spherical loom**, **Eight rooms of light**, **An octahedron within**, **Section garden**, and **An orthogonal shadow**. Colors identify original coordinate directions for edge and grid lines, or the original cell axis for sections. They are not a depth or curvature measurement. A section edge shared by two cells takes the first cell's color in deterministic axis order.

## Controls and motion

Every angle and geometric scalar accepts constants and arithmetic, including `pi`, `e`, and `phi`, evaluated in Go. Angles are radians. A positive rotation in ij carries +i toward +j. Rotations act in order xy, xz, yz, xw, yw, zw; these rotations generally do not commute.

A flat coordinate diagram has neither an indicator nor named-view buttons of its own and remains unchanged by a paired named view; in the shell bypass, only the shadow turns. **Rotate view** slowly orbits the three-dimensional camera; **Reset view** restores its default orientation, pan, and zoom. Scale is equal on all axes. The fixed framing sphere contains every orientation, preventing camera breathing during playback. Stereographic framing uses the explicitly chosen window radius. Pan and zoom apply on top of it and persist during playback.

The **Animation** panel, open by default, offers an **Animate** dropdown adapted to the current construction. Rotation choices are available for tesseract studies; **Slice passage** appears for cross-sections. Animation adds a full turn in xw, or in both xw and yz, to the entered angles. This is a change to those two angles in the stated composition, not an additional global rotation after all six. Tesseract slice passage moves h linearly from −2.05 to 2.05 for one section, and for a family from −(2.05 + spread/2) to 2.05 + spread/2, so its nearest section also passes 2.05 and every section is empty at both ends. Curved passage uses the support and family spread described below. **Repeat** plays once, as a **Loop**, or **Back and forth**, and **Pace** keeps it steady or eases it in and out (see [mathematics](mathematics.md#seamless-loops)). Other motions offer back and forth. **Hopf circles, flowing forever** loops Hopf fibers through a double rotation. Playback can pause/resume or scrub, and a single pass includes both endpoints. A quick scrub draws each frame the engine finishes, as playback does, and settles on the last position; as in the 3D notebook, a frame catching up with the scrubber leaves **Copy link**, **Export image** and the drawing undisturbed. An edit, Play or Stop discards any frame still being computed. Once playback completes, the view is released: orbit, pan, zoom, **Rotate view**, and **Reset view** work on the final frame. **Stop** (or **Back to study** after completion) restores the entered study; edits and notebook switches stop playback. An empty section is a valid result.

**Line weight**, after the layers, draws every stroke fine, regular (as before), bold, or as a one-pixel hairline, live and in every export, as in the 2D notebook; links carry it inside `layers`, and older links open regular. Playback calculates each frame, and on a computer with spare cores a second engine shares that work while it plays.

PNG exports render vectors at 2000 × 1520, or at 1, 3 or 4 times the 1000 × 760 page under **Export image → PNG size**; a paired view's page is 2000 × 760 or 1000 × 1520, so its default PNG is 4000 × 1520 or 2000 × 3040. **Transparent background** leaves the background out of the PNG and SVG, the page's and each panel's. Their page has the still's size (the PNG size), and its root carries the background, so a viewer shows the drawing and nothing beyond it, as for a 3D export; a size other than the default is recorded in the metadata as `image`. Motion exports use the same numerical sampler and vector drawing as playback, as MP4 or animated WebP when the browser supports encoding. **Export settings** offers resolution from 500 × 380 to 2000 × 1520, quality from 1 to 100, and 15, 30 or 60 fps (a 60 fps WebP is about twice the size of 30). Defaults match the other notebooks: 2000 × 1520, 30 fps, quality 60 for MP4 or 85 for WebP. ## Mathematics and numerical limits

The model is C = [−1, 1]⁴. Its 16 vertices have four signs; changing one sign gives one of 32 edges. Choosing two free coordinates and two fixed signs gives its 24 square faces. Fixing one coordinate at ±1 gives one of its eight cubic cells. Circumradius is 2 and edge length is 2.

For rotated v, orthographic projection is v_xyz. Perspective projection is d v_xyz / (d − v_w). The eye distance is restricted to 2.05 ≤ d ≤ 20, so the projection plane cannot meet the object during any rotation. A containing sphere for the projected object has radius 2d / sqrt(d² − 4), obtained by maximizing projected distance on the 4D circum-sphere.

A section w = h intersects each cell's twelve edges. Coplanar endpoints are retained, coincident points are deduplicated, and each planar polygon is ordered in its own plane. Coincident polygons and shared edges are deduplicated. A cubic cell lying entirely in the section plane is three-dimensional and is not mistaken for a polygon: its boundary comes from adjacent cells. Tangential points and segments are retained and reported; an empty section is reported explicitly. Coordinate tolerance is 10⁻¹⁰ for the plane and 10⁻⁹ for point identity and affine dimension, relative to the fixed unit cube. Features within that tolerance coalesce. Section offsets are within ±4.05, the end of the widest family's passage, spread within [0, 4], and count within [1, 25].

For stereographic projection, p = v/|v| and P(p) = p_xyz/(1 − p_w). A straight source segment lies in a two-plane through the origin, so its radial normalization is a great-circle arc of S³. Its image is a circle or a line in R³. The source never passes through the origin: at least two face coordinates are ±1.

The finite window |P(p)| ≤ R is equivalent to p_w ≤ (R² − 1)/(R² + 1). Along a source segment v(t), crossing this boundary reduces to a quadratic in t. Its roots split the segment **before sampling**, so even a pole between two samples cannot be bridged. Extraneous roots with negative w are rejected. The window radius is 2–12; clipped source-curve counts are reported. Kept intervals are sampled using 8–256 subdivisions of the original segment, with at least two subdivisions per retained piece. Increasing samples improves the circular-arc approximation; it does not change topology or pole clipping.

A grid adds 0–12 parallel lines per direction per face, or 48g source lines beyond the 32 true edges. There are at most 608 source curves and at most two retained pieces per curve. No surface mesh, scene topology, or 4D lighting is inferred. Transparent SVG faces use average-depth ordering for illustration; this is not exact opaque hidden-surface rendering. Curved paths are polylines, with second-order chord convergence. No geometry is interpolated between animation frames.

## Implementation and verification

`engine4` is a pure Go package, separate from `engine` and `engine3`. Its JSON bridge in `cmd/wasm` runs in the existing browser worker. `web/tesseract` owns controls and the ordinary 3D viewing camera. One active numerical request and one replaceable waiting request bound playback and scrubbing work; revision tokens reject stale results. Export computes and encodes one frame at a time using the existing bounded encoders. There are no new runtime dependencies or resource types.

Native tests check rotation isometry, edge counts, perspective formulas and bounds, cube and octahedron sections, tangent and empty sections, Euler identities, inverse-rotated cube membership, stereographic inverse identities, poles between samples, chord convergence, limits and invalid input. The real WASM bridge checks the independent request/result contract. Browser tests cover examples, scalar expressions and superseded edits, playback and endpoints, notebook and camera preservation, image exports, independently decoded MP4/WebP timing, cancellation, paired-field layout, desktop and phone widths, and both themes.

## What the notebook does not model

The curved studies below (the ring solid, the thread missing from a slice, the route beside the wall, the ring weaves) answer geometric questions prompted by scenes in Liu Cixin's _Death's End_. They are not a claim about physical four-dimensional space. These ideas were considered and left out, because the available geometry does not support them:

- **A bubble as a conventional lens.** A 3D refractive sphere models optical distortion, not invisibility in three dimensions. There is no 4D optical interface or observer model to extend the spatial Snell-law engine with.
- **An unoccluded drawing as literal four-dimensional vision.** A flat display of finite curves cannot show every interior point distinctly. Sparse or dense layers explain chosen structure; they do not simulate a 4D retina.
- **Abrupt size jumps from a hidden coordinate.** A smooth path under a regular perspective projection changes continuously. Poles, occlusion or deliberately discontinuous observations would need their own definitions.
- **Four-dimensional volume flattening into a luminous line.** A volume-preserving linear map stays full rank while its determinant is nonzero, and a zero-width limit at fixed volume has unbounded extent. Nor does a shrinking compact circle (`R³ × S¹`) derive a decay mechanism.
- **Signal falloff and inferred physics.** Flux spreading over `(n−1)`-spheres says nothing about coupling between dimensions; physical rates and distances are not simulation parameters.

More regular polytopes would fit the notebook, but only as their own geometric study, not to lengthen the shape menu.

## Exact curved sections

Choose **4D object** or the **A sphere in passing** and **A ring in passing** examples. Object choice and view operation are independent, with only supported combinations offered. Switching restores each object's last valid definition, including the tesseract's angles, projection and grid settings; a first visit uses support-derived defaults. Object replacement stops playback and discards outstanding scalar evaluations. The ball and circular tube support axis-aligned `w = h` sections, singly or in a finite family. Their 4D rotation fields and projection modes are unavailable. Dragging still orbits the ordinary 3D drawing. All radii, offsets and spreads accept constant expressions through Go's scalar parser.

The centered 4-ball is `x²+y²+z²+w² ≤ R²`. Its section is a solid 3-ball of radius `ρ = sqrt(R²−h²)` inside `|h| < R`, a point at tangency, and empty outside. Representative meridians and latitudes illustrate the boundary 2-sphere; the section is three-dimensional even though each drawn circle is one-dimensional.

The **Circular 4D tube** is `(sqrt(x²+y²)−R)²+z²+w² ≤ r²`, with `0 < r < R`. It is a solid with topology `S¹ × B³`, whose boundary is `S¹ × S²`, rather than a Clifford torus. Inside `|h| < r`, its section is a solid torus of tube radius `ρ = sqrt(r²−h²)`, with fixed core radius R. Boundary curves sample `((R+ρ cos v)cos u, (R+ρ cos v)sin u, ρ sin v)` at fixed u and v. At tangency the section is the entire core circle, of dimension 1; outside it is empty. This is one mathematical ring model, not an identification of a fictional artifact.

Sections overlay their original xyz coordinates. They are neither translated nor connected into a surface. Opposite offsets have coincident geometry but distinct `section/N` identities and explicit level readouts. The compact key links each section number and color to its signed h. Its responsive grid reserves space for the number, signed value and stroke label; tabular digits keep the readouts steady. Camera help occupies a separate row, so changing signs or magnitudes cannot move its line breaks during passage. Negative levels use dashed strokes and nonnegative levels use solid strokes, so opposite coincident outlines remain distinguishable. **Selected section** (numbered from 1) strengthens that outline and dims the others; its non-live readout gives h, kind, dimension and radius. The readout stays in the surrounding UI during passage and scrubbing. The renderable viewport contains geometry only, with no visible text; this also applies to thumbnails and every export. Selection styling persists in SVG/PNG and motion exports, with the selected index recorded in layer metadata. Family live diagnostics announce only the intersection summary. These drawings omit interior points, unsampled levels and fourth-coordinate separation. No polyhedral vertex, edge or face counts are fabricated for smooth sections.

The ball's fixed framing radius is R; the tube's is R+r. Let S be R for the ball or r for the tube. Slice passage samples h linearly from −E to E, where `E = 1.025 S + spread/2` for a family, or `1.025 S` for one section. The 2.5% relative margin makes every section empty at both family endpoints. Playback and exports use the same immutable sampler and vector renderer. Only h is animated in this pass. Radius tracks and tilted sections are not supported.

Supported radii are 0.001–100 for R and 0.001 ≤ r < R for the tube. Offset is within ±4S and family spread within [0,4S] (a single section ignores finite spread), with 1–25 sections, 3–16 curves per direction, and 8–256 subdivisions per circle. Ball sections use `2c−1` circles (c meridians and c−1 interior latitudes); tube sections use `2c`. Counts share ceilings of 512 source curves and 65,536 emitted points per entire family. A conservative preflight applies to all requested levels, including empty levels, and rejects the whole request before allocation when it exceeds a ceiling. There are no root refinements. Each emitted non-closure sample uses one evaluator call, so the point ceiling also bounds evaluator work; the result reports both actual counts.

Tangency is decided using absolute-plus-relative coordinate tolerance `τ = 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ S`: `S−|h| < −τ` is empty, within ±τ collapses to the point/core circle, and above τ is a solid section. The radius is evaluated stably as `sqrt((S−|h|)(S+|h|))`. Within that tolerance, tiny features intentionally coalesce; no approximation claims exact topology below τ. Circle endpoints are copied exactly, and chords converge quadratically with subdivisions.

For curved objects, stable path identity consists of object/source (`ball/meridian/k`, `ball/latitude/k`, `tube/fixed-u/k`, `tube/fixed-v/k`, or `tube/core`), section ID and branch `0`, with role `section`. Sections carry their h, dimension, kind (`solid`, `point`, `core-circle`, `empty`) and boundary radius. Point contacts retain identity in the section diagnostics. SVG metadata records object, operation, levels, radii, parameters, camera, layers and omitted information. Native and real-WASM tests verify analytic equations, radii, degeneration, scale/tolerance boundaries, identities, budget rejection and convergence. Tests integrate section volumes from the engine's returned radii: `4πρ³/3` and `2π²Rρ²`, comparing against the independent 4D identities `π²R⁴/2` and `8π²Rr³/3`. Circumference and sagitta tests verify quadratic convergence for meridians, latitudes, fixed-u and fixed-v circles. Tesseract path indices are rendering keys, not stable geometric identities across rotations or levels.

## Localized thread lift

Choose **Localized thread lift** or **The missing middle**. Five straight sources are Cᵢ(t) = (−3+6t, (i−2)/2, 0), i = 0…4, and the circular strand is C(t) = (1.75 cos(2πt), 1.75 sin(2πt), 0.5), all with t ∈ [0,1]. The circle's endpoints coincide exactly. Screen crossings never create connections between sources.

For p = (x,y,z), center c, support radius L and height A, the compact lift is H(p) = A(1−|p−c|²/L²)² inside |p−c| < L and zero outside. Its first derivative is continuous at the support boundary. The modeled slab is |w−H(p)| ≤ ε with fixed half-thickness **ε = 0.02**. This thickness is a modeling choice in source-coordinate units. The support radius L is distinct from the derived missing radius r = L sqrt(1−sqrt(ε/A)) when A > ε; r is zero otherwise. At w = 0, H ≤ ε is present, including equality.

**Reference slice** draws only present intervals at w = 0. **Lifted construction** draws each entire connected centerline (C(t),H(C(t))) using the declared xw presentation rotation followed by orthographic xyz projection: (x cos θ − w sin θ, y, z). This extra-coordinate view explains the embedding. Its angle is ignored in reference mode. A positive xw rotation carries +x toward +w. The ordinary orbit camera acts afterward. This construction generally changes lengths and makes no claim about preserved tension, flow, survival or fictional physics.

Optional **Missing-region guide** draws three circles of radius r about c. **Displacement connectors**, available in the construction view, join (p,0) to (p,H(p)) at nine representative parameters per source when H is nonzero. They indicate displacement, not additional material or light rays. Geometry, guides and their visibility are shared by still and motion exports. Legend, camera help and numerical readouts occupy the surrounding UI; the viewport and exported drawing contain no visible text. Readouts use fixed columns and tabular digits. The live region gives a constant connectivity summary rather than announcing each changing value.

Center and drift endpoint coordinates are within ±20; support and its motion endpoints are 0.05–20; height is 0–10. The construction angle is within ±1,000,000 radians; reference mode ignores it. All these fields accept constant expressions. Center, drift-start and drift-end coordinates each occupy a labeled x/y/z row; support and height have their own row. Validation names the active field; lift evaluation ignores the tesseract rotation fields. **Thread samples** chooses 8–256 subdivisions of the original parameter interval. **Move lift center** interpolates the entered start/end centers. **Change lift support** interpolates the entered start/end radii; reversing those endpoints reverses the motion. ### Clipping, identities and work bounds

Go partitions the parameter interval before sampling. Straight-source sphere crossings use closest-point geometry and quadratic roots; circle crossings solve a cosine equation analytically. Exact crossing parameters, support crossings and extrema supplement uniform subdivisions, so a bump or missing interval between samples is retained. Entirely absent intervals stay absent. Tangent contacts survive as round-capped zero-length paths with duplicated coordinates. The present set is closed: pieces and cut points are present when `|C(t)−c|² ≥ r² − 10⁻¹² r (r + √10)`, a squared-distance tolerance proportional to the rounding of that distance near the boundary (√10 bounds every source point, and hence |c| wherever contact is possible). Without it, a tangency could split into a spurious chord a few ulps wide, and a circle lying on the missing boundary could collapse to one point. Any chord deeper than that tolerance is still cut; tests check one reaching only 10⁻⁷ r inside missing radii of 0.09 and larger, and tangencies with missing radii near 10⁻⁶. A circle crossing the parameter seam retains two drawing pieces to avoid bridging the missing arc, but **Present intervals** counts the connected arc once: t = 0 and t = 1 identify the same source point. A solitary seam contact is emitted once at canonical t = 0; a seam contact adjoining a present arc belongs to that arc. Open line endpoints remain distinct. The lifted circle is always one connected source path.

Line-root residual tolerance is 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ max(1,r²); cosine roots clamp within 10⁻¹² of ±1. Contact membership allows 10⁻²⁴ + 10⁻¹²r² in squared distance. Interior midpoint membership uses the strict squared-distance comparison, so a fixed absolute tolerance cannot join a known tiny gap. Parameters within 10⁻¹³ coalesce. These rules acknowledge floating-point boundary uncertainty; they do not claim exact topology below parameter resolution. No iterative root isolation or refinement is needed for the fixed source set.

Sources are `lift/thread/0` through `lift/thread/4` and `lift/circle`; guides are `lift/missing-guide/0` through `/2`. Path identity combines source, role (`reference`, `lifted`, `displacement`, `missing-guide`) and ordered branch. Role is part of the render key because a connector and its centerline share a source. Source paths carry corresponding parameter values and four-dimensional coordinates; reference coordinates have w = 0, while construction coordinates carry the evaluated H. Diagnostics report six sources, connected present-interval and absent-source counts, ε, missing radius and projection. SVG metadata retains the definition, identities, intervals, framing, layers and camera.

The entire fixed family conservatively fits within 75 paths, 2,500 emitted points and 2,700 source/guide evaluator calls, including classification, duplicated contacts, connectors and guides at maximum sampling. These are whole-study bounds, not per-curve allowances. Algebraic root solves are bounded by six sources; there is no iterative refinement count. Returned counts and regression assertions verify emitted work. Chord-error tests measure returned geometry against independent analytic midpoints and verify second-order convergence; native tests also cover exact endpoints, contacts, missing intervals, large-boundary tangent-plane scaling, validation and reference/construction membership.

The first implementation supports only these analytic sources and w = 0. Nonzero slice levels require a different membership operation that can select radial bands. Arbitrary input curves, overlapping lifts, filled bubble surfaces, a moving marker, a cage preset and physical simulation remain outside this study.

## Shell bypass

Choose **Shell bypass** or **Beside the wall**. This study compares a verified route around an embedded enclosure with a radial enclosure in four dimensions. It is a geometric construction, with no physical collision simulation or general obstacle editor.

The embedded obstacle is the closed set `K = {(p,w): a ≤ |p| ≤ b, |w| ≤ ε}`, where p = (x,y,z), 0 < a < b and ε ≥ 0. The route has four exact vertices `(p_out,0)`, `(p_out,H)`, `(0,H)`, `(0,0)`, with D = |p_out| > b. Each linear leg occupies one third of s ∈ [0,1]. The first stays outside the shell in xyz, the middle passes above it in w, and the final leg returns inside the cavity. In exact arithmetic H > ε clears K; H = ε contacts its closed boundary; H < ε crosses it. The minimum Euclidean distance to K for a clear route is `min(D−b, a, H−ε)`. Contacts and crossings report zero clearance. For ε = H = 0, the middle leg crosses the lower-dimensional shell.

**Radial 4D shell** instead uses `K₄ = {q: a ≤ |q| ≤ b}`. Every continuous route from an outside point to the origin meets this shell, because its radial distance passes through [a,b]. Increasing H does not evade it. The defining inequality establishes containment; the sparse drawing alone does not.

**XYZ shadow** drops w and applies the ordinary 3D orbit camera. Its circles outline the obstacle's own XYZ shadow. The shadow of K is the 3D shell `a ≤ |p| ≤ b`, which is also its w = 0 section, so six circles mark both radii. The shadow of K₄ is the filled ball `|p| ≤ b`: a cavity point (p,0) with |p| < a lifts to (p,w) with `a² ≤ |p|²+w² ≤ b²`, so it belongs to the shadow. Only three outer circles are drawn, and the legend omits the inner boundary there. Drawing the w = 0 section of K₄ in a shadow would show a cavity the shadow does not have. **Coordinate diagram** uses horizontal radial position |p| and vertical w with equal scale; its rectangular embedded-shell outline or two radial-shell semicircles represent the defining inequalities. It is a coordinate diagram, not another 3D camera. Dragging and arrows pan this view; zoom remains available. Retained yaw/pitch have no effect on it and return when switching to the shadow. Linked views keep the same s, moving point and physical diagnostics. Each representation has fixed framing throughout traversal: radius D for the shadow, and `hypot(D/2, (w_hi−w_lo)/2)` about `(D/2, (w_hi+w_lo)/2)` for the diagram. The w bounds contain the route, comparison points and obstacle. They do not depend on s.

Choose **View operation → Side-by-side views** to see the XYZ shadow and coordinate diagram together. The option is off by default. One worker result supplies both panels at the same route position; playback, scrubbing and reverse traversal update them together. Each panel has its own camera: orbit/pan/zoom the shadow and pan/zoom the diagram. Switching to the diagram alone and back preserves both cameras. Numbered captions name each panel's position (left/right, or top/bottom when stacked) and, like the interaction help, remain outside the drawing. At narrow widths the panels and their captions stack, preserving readable scale while continuing to animate together. A shadow camera stored while viewing the diagram alone returns only when you go straight back to side-by-side; choosing the shadow alone in between keeps the most recent camera instead.

SVG, PNG, MP4 and WebP capture both panels, their independent cameras and the layout displayed when export starts: columns on a wide window, rows below 700 px. Resizing across that width therefore changes the exported aspect ratio. Each panel retains its 1000 × 760 coordinate system. At 100% resolution, columns export at 2000 × 760 and stacked rows at 1000 × 1520; the default 200% doubles both dimensions. The 7,200-frame limit is unchanged. Export renders one frame canvas at a time and holds the encoded file in memory until it is saved; a paired frame has twice the pixels of a single view, so canvas memory and encoded size are roughly doubled for the same duration. Existing single-view exports keep their dimensions.

A faint dashed context shows the entire route; the traveled prefix ends at the exact moving point. A clear prefix is solid; contact/crossing prefixes are dashed, with red intersection intervals. Full-route classification remains visible even before the marker reaches a blocked interval. Layer controls independently show the route/marker, shell/guides and comparison points. Current w, route state, clearance, legend and interaction help stay outside the renderable viewport. Still images, thumbnails and animations contain geometry only.

The optional comparison points are `q₁ = (a/2,0,0,w₁)` and `q₂ = (a/2,0,0,w₂)`. Small and larger rings retain separate identities even where the XYZ shadows coincide. Expanding **Same shadow, different points** reveals both w coordinates, zero XYZ separation and 4D separation `sqrt(|Δp|²+Δw²) = |w₂−w₁|`. The coordinate diagram separates their w values. This explains discarded information; it is not a distance inference from a screen image.

All geometric fields use the constant-expression parser. Limits are a = 0.05–10, b ≤ 20 with b−a ≥ 0.001, embedded ε = 0–5, H = 0–20, s = 0–1, each outside coordinate within ±20, D > b and D ≤ 40, and w₁/w₂ within ±20. The radial shell hides and ignores ε, including unfinished values; tesseract rotation fields are inactive for both representations. **Shell samples** is a whole number from 8 to 256, affecting boundary circles only. **Traverse route** samples s from 0 to 1; **Return along route** samples 1 to 0. Stop restores the entered s and manual camera. Both motions use the same immutable numerical sampler and renderer for playback, SVG/PNG and MP4/WebP exports.

### Intersection events, identities and bounds

Go partitions each complete segment at inner/outer sphere roots and, for K, both slab planes. Each resulting piece is classified at its own midpoint before adjacent pieces merge, so a tangency inside a merged interval cannot hide a crossing; closed endpoints retain isolated contacts. A positive-length piece is a crossing when its midpoint is strictly interior, otherwise boundary contact (for example, a w-fiber along the cavity wall |p| = a). This does not depend on shell sampling or the displayed prefix. Sphere roots use closest-point geometry with squared residual tolerance `10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹² r²`; a residual within that tolerance is one tangent event, because the root half-width grows like its square root and rounding would otherwise split a tangency into a spurious chord; endpoints use coordinate membership tolerance `τ = 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ b`. Interior membership is strict. Parameters within 10⁻¹³ coalesce. Near a boundary, τ conservatively retains contacts; the exact clearance criterion above does not certify distinctions below this floating-point resolution. Tangencies and degenerate legs remain explicit. There are six analytic sphere solves for the three route legs, with no iterative root refinement.

Stable identities are `bypass/route` (role `route-context`), `bypass/traveled` (`route-traveled`), `bypass/hit/i` (`collision`), `bypass/shell/i/plane` or `bypass/shell/i` (`shell`), and `bypass/radial-axis` / `bypass/w-axis` (`axes`). Branch is `0`. Route paths retain corresponding global s parameters and original four-dimensional coordinates. Markers `bypass/moving`, `bypass/q1` and `bypass/q2` carry projected and physical coordinates and roles. Diagnostics carry the complete intersection intervals, state, clearance, current point/s and separations; SVG metadata retains them with definition, framing, representation, layers and manual camera.

Each representation has conservative bounds of 24 paths, 2,000 emitted points including markers, and 2,500 projection/membership evaluations. A paired result is bounded by 48 paths, 4,000 emitted points and 5,000 evaluations across both panels; its top-level counters include companion work. Sample limits are validated before allocation. Whole-segment event tests cover reversal, tangency, isolated and boundary-fiber contact, slab crossings and the radial fourth-axis interval. Because both obstacles are canonical, a common rigid motion of route and obstacle is tested through each obstacle's symmetries, which move the route and fix the obstacle: rotations of xyz and w ↦ −w for K, every rotation of R⁴ for K₄. An independent membership test checks every returned interval and the gaps between them. Clearance is compared with an independently measured distance from the densely sampled complete route to K, and 200 deterministic polylines with arbitrary corners confirm that no outside-to-origin route evades K₄. Returned-geometry tests independently verify physical norms, marker projections and separations, hit endpoints and framing. Actual circle chords show quadratic convergence. The real WASM bridge verifies the same contract; browser tests cover controls, linked progress, scalar invalidation, restoration, canceled exports and independently decoded live/export parity.

## Spherical ring weave

Choose **Spherical ring weave**, **Rings from a sphere** (Hopf fibers) or **Tori between two circles** (Clifford tori). Every source curve lies on the unit 3-sphere S³ in R⁴. The drawing is its stereographic projection; open ends mark the projection window, not broken curves. Neither a Clifford torus nor a Hopf fiber is a sealed 4D solid.

**Clifford tori.** For latitude 0 ≤ α ≤ π/2, q(u,v,α) = (cos α cos u, cos α sin u, sin α cos v, sin α sin v). For 0 < α < π/2 this is a torus with |(x,y)| = cos α and |(z,w)| = sin α; α = π/4 is the Clifford torus with equal radii. The drawing keeps m **fixed-u** circles (v varies) and m **fixed-v** circles (u varies) per latitude, at u, v = 2πj/m.

**Hopf fibers.** With z₁ = x + iy and z₂ = z + iw, each fiber is the great circle (e^{it}z₁, e^{it}z₂). The fiber at latitude α and phase δ = u − v is q(t) = (cos α cos(t+δ), cos α sin(t+δ), sin α cos t, sin α sin t), with m fibers per latitude at δ = 2πj/m. The Hopf map H(z₁,z₂) = (2 Re z₁z̄₂, 2 Im z₁z̄₂, |z₁|² − |z₂|²) sends this whole fiber to (sin 2α cos δ, sin 2α sin δ, cos 2α) on S². Fibers over one latitude lie on its torus, and any two distinct complete fibers link once.

**Latitudes.** n latitudes (1–9) are αₖ = α + s(k/(n−1) − ½), with central latitude α and spread s; a single latitude is α and ignores spread. Every latitude, at the entered α and at both motion endpoints, must lie within 0 and π/2, and s must be positive when n > 1. Latitudes within 10⁻¹² of 0 or π/2 snap to that endpoint. There the torus collapses to one circle, the xy unit circle at α = 0 or the zw circle at α = π/2. Every fixed-v circle or fiber would coincide with it, and every fixed-u circle would shrink to a point, so it is drawn once as a **collapsed** source, and the latitude reports kind `circle` and dimension 1. A small positive α such as 10⁻⁶ remains a thin torus.

**Rotation and projection.** The six angles rotate the whole sphere in the stated plane order before projection, as for the tesseract; the camera acts afterwards. Stereographic projection from the pole (0,0,0,1) is P(q) = q_xyz/(1 − q_w). A circle on S³ projects to a circle, or to a line when it passes through the pole. The window |P| ≤ C, with 2 ≤ C ≤ 12, is exactly q_w ≤ k = (C² − 1)/(C² + 1) on S³. The framing radius is C, and it is constant through every motion. Optional **Projection window** guides draw three great circles of |P| = C.

### Window clipping, identities and bounds

Each source is a circle q(t) = c + a cos t + b sin t in R⁴, rotated as vectors. Its fourth coordinate is q_w(t) = c_w + ρ cos(t − φ), with ρ = hypot(a_w, b_w) and φ = atan2(b_w, a_w). Go classifies it **before sampling**, with tolerance τ = 10⁻¹² in q_w:

- **Complete.** max q_w = c_w + ρ ≤ k + τ, which includes an internal tangency.
- **Outside the window.** min q_w > k + τ.
- **Window contact.** k − τ ≤ min q_w, with max q_w > k + τ. Only the lowest point t = φ + π meets the window. It is kept as a round-capped zero-length path.
- **Open arc.** Otherwise, the retained arc is t ∈ [φ + h, φ + 2π − h], with h = acos((k − c_w)/ρ).

In the open-arc case both crossings lie at least √(2τ/ρ) from the maximum, so `acos` is well conditioned. A crossing within τ of a tangency coalesces into it instead of leaving a sliver arc or a spurious gap. The window is one sinusoid threshold, so each source has at most one retained arc. A pole between two samples can never be bridged. Tests sweep 400 rotations for internal tangency, and 1,200 for the external case. They also check that a crossing 10⁻⁹ deep is still cut, and that an arc 10⁻⁶ inside a lower tangency keeps an interior sample.

Retained arcs use the source's own subdivision grid t = 2πj/N, for N arc samples (8–256), plus both exact crossings. A retained arc always has at least two subdivisions. A complete circle has N + 1 points; its last point repeats the first exactly. Parameters are angles in radians, unwrapped along the retained arc. Chords converge to the projected circle at second order.

Sources are `weave/latitude/k/fixed-u/j`, `weave/latitude/k/fixed-v/j`, `weave/latitude/k/fiber/j` and `weave/latitude/k/core`, with roles `fixed-u`, `fixed-v`, `fiber` and `collapsed`. Window guides are `weave/window/xy`, `/yz` and `/zx`, with role `window`. Each source path carries section id `weave/latitude/k` and branch 0, together with parameters and rotated four-dimensional coordinates. Result sections record each latitude's α as `level`, its kind (`torus` or `circle`) and its dimension.

The status summary is constant for a definition: the number of source circles, and a statement that open ends mark the window. Readouts outside the viewport report complete circles, open arcs, window contacts, sources outside the window and the threshold k. `clipped` counts sources with any part removed. SVG metadata records the definition, diagnostics, arcs (source, latitude, role and parameter ends), framing, layers and camera. Each latitude has its own color and α in the key, and fixed-u and fixed-v circles share it. Screen crossings are not intersections. A clipped arc does not have the linking number of its complete circle.

The budget is decided from the counts before evaluation: n × (circles per latitude) × (N + 2) + 3(N + 1) ≤ **65,536 points**. There are 2m circles per latitude for tori and m for fibers, with m from 1 to 16. Evaluations equal emitted points: one per source or guide point, and no iterative root finding.

**Sweep latitudes** moves the central α linearly from **Latitude start** to **Latitude end**, holding spread and rotation fixed. It reaches both endpoints exactly. **Double rotation** and **One plane · xw** add full turns to the entered angles, as for the tesseract. The window stays fixed, so circles open into arcs and close again as they pass the pole. Stop restores the entered study and camera.

Validation names the field: weave family, rotation angles, **Latitudes**, **Curves per direction** or **Fibers per latitude**, **Arc samples**, **Projection window radius**, **Latitude spread**, **Central latitude α**, **Latitude start** and **Latitude end**. An over-budget request is rejected with the budget in the message.

### Base sphere and fibers

Choose **Base sphere and fibers** under **View operation**, or open **A circle over every point**. Two panes share one result. **Base sphere S²** (left, or on top on a phone) draws a path on the base sphere, its sampled points and a moving point. **Fibers in space** (right, or below) draws the Hopf fiber over each of those points, stereographically projected through the same window as the weave. **Base path** chooses **Latitude** (the default) or **Spherical flower**. The mode always draws Hopf fibers on one path, so the family, **Latitudes** and **Latitude spread** do not apply and are hidden; switching back to **Stereographic loom** restores them.

**The latitude and its samples.** The base point at phase δ is p(δ) = (sin 2α cos δ, sin 2α sin δ, cos 2α), with α the **Central latitude α** (0 to π/2) of the weave. **Fibers on the latitude**, n from 1 to 32, places samples at δⱼ = 2πj/n. Sample j's point and fiber share the identity j: its base marker is `weave/latitude/0/base/j` and its fiber `weave/latitude/0/fiber/j`. The latitude itself is `weave/latitude/0/base`, with role `base-curve`, sampled at the arc samples and closed exactly. Three great circles `weave/globe/xy`, `/yz` and `/zx` (role `globe`) and the camera's outline of the unit sphere are guides.

**From a point to its fiber.** Every fiber is built from its base point, independently of the path formula. With z₁ = x + iy and z₂ = z + iw, H(q) = p requires |z₁|² = (1 + p_z)/2, |z₂|² = (1 − p_z)/2 and z₁z̄₂ = (p_x + i p_y)/2. Two overlapping charts choose a representative q:

- **North** (defined for p_z > −1): z₁ = √((1 + p_z)/2), real and positive, and z₂ = (p_x − i p_y)/(2z₁).
- **South** (defined for p_z < 1): z₂ = √((1 − p_z)/2), real and positive, and z₁ = (p_x + i p_y)/(2z₂).

The engine uses north for p_z ≥ 0 and south otherwise. The chart's real coordinate is then at least 1/√2, so neither pole divides by a small number, and q is normalized to |q| = 1. Where both charts are defined, q_S = e^{iδ} q_N with δ = arg(p_x + i p_y). Both are the same fiber e^{it}q, with its parameter t shifted by δ. A change of chart reparameterizes a circle but never changes the circle, so a seam in the phase is not a gap. Tests compare fibers as sets (the same great-circle plane), never by their t origins. At p = (0, 0, 1) the fiber is the xy unit circle and at (0, 0, −1) the zw circle, as for the weave's collapsed latitudes. A sample's fiber matches the ordinary weave's fiber at the same α and δⱼ as a set, with the same window classification. Where α < π/4 its parameter origin differs, so its arc parameters differ by δⱼ.

**Selection and the moving point.** **Selected fiber**, from 1 to n, is saved with the study as `weave.selected` (0-based, absent as 0). Tapping or clicking in either pane selects the sample whose drawn fiber or point lies nearest, within 24 screen pixels, measured by distance rather than by the topmost element. A drag orbits instead. With a pane focused, `[` and `]` step the selection. Lowering the fiber count below the selection selects the last fiber. The moving point lies at δ = δ_selected + 2π·frac(trace), where only **Trace the latitude** sets the trace (one turn over the motion), so a link never carries it. At a whole number of turns the moving point is the selected sample. Its fiber is then that sample's fiber, drawn once in the accent ink, and the result reports `movingSample` as the selection. Between samples, Go adds `weave/moving` (role `moving`) and the base marker `weave/moving/base`, and `movingSample` is −1. The moving fiber at δ equal to a sample's δⱼ is that sample's fiber bit for bit, and a whole turn returns the whole result bit for bit, so the trace loops.

**Collapsed latitudes.** At α = 0 or π/2, after the weave's 10⁻¹² snap, every sample and the moving point are the pole. The bouquet then has one circle, `weave/latitude/0/core` (role `collapsed`), and the base pane one marker, `weave/latitude/0/base/core`. The diagnostics keep the selected index and report `coincident`.

**Rotation.** The six angles rotate the fibers before projection, exactly as in the weave. The base sphere is not rotated: it shows H of each unrotated fiber, which a general 4D rotation does not preserve. Window classification, contacts, coalescence and clipping are the weave's, applied to each fiber circle before sampling. Clipping can open a fiber into an arc; that is the window, not a break, and a clipped arc carries no linking claim.

**Results.** The bouquet is the result (operation `paired`); the base sphere is its `companion` (operation `base`, framing radius 1.3). One request computes both, so a frame cannot pair one moment's base point with another's fiber. Both carry `weave.correspondence`: `fibers`, `selected`, `movingSample`, the moving point's `parameter` δ in [0, 2π), its `base` point, its `chart` (`north` or `south`) and `coincident`. Base markers carry the point as `point` and its unit representative q as `fourPoint`. Readouts outside the viewport report the selection, δ and the coordinates of p with the window counts. The status line names only the number of fibers, so it does not change during a trace.

**Bounds.** The points are counted before evaluation: (n + 1)(N + 2) + 3(N + 1) in space, plus (N + 1) + 3(N + 1) + n + 1 on the base sphere, for N arc samples. The largest admitted study, 32 fibers and 256 samples, counts 10,346 of the 65,536 points, so the counts alone keep every admitted study inside the ceiling; the check stays in place in case the counts grow. Evaluations equal emitted points. Validation names **Fibers on the latitude** (1–32), **Selected fiber** (1 to n), **Arc samples**, **Projection window radius**, the rotation angles, **Central latitude α**, **Latitude start** and **Latitude end**. Each must lie in 0 to π/2. A link must leave the trace at 0.

**Motion and exports.** **Trace the latitude** moves the point once around from the selected sample and loops. **Double rotation**, **One plane · xw** and **Sweep latitudes** are offered as in the weave; the base sphere moves only with the latitude. The two panes share one timeline. Each has its own camera (`view` for the fibers, `diagramView` for the base sphere), and playback holds both. Stills, motion exports and the loop check use the shared paired composition: 2000 × 760 in columns, or 1000 × 1520 in rows on a phone, base pane first, with the pane names outside the artwork. A gallery thumbnail shows the fibers' pane alone.

**Checks.** `engine4/hopf_test.go` covers H(q) = p and |q| = 1 at both poles, near them and on the chart boundary, and the overlap relation q_S = e^{iδ}q_N. It checks every returned unrotated fiber point and representative against its own sample, and set equality and identical classification with the ordinary weave latitude. It verifies the linking number ±1, with one orientation, on every pair of complete fibers in safe views. That test uses an exact polygon formula (the signed solid angles of segment pairs), independent of the engine. It also covers coincidence with a sample, whole-turn identity, collapsed latitudes, validation and the exact point count at the largest study.

### Spherical flower

**A flower below, a bouquet above** opens the same paired workspace with **Spherical flower** selected. Its authored path is

`α(t) = α₀ + A cos(kt)`,

`p(t) = (sin(2α(t)) cos t, sin(2α(t)) sin t, cos(2α(t)))`, for `0 ≤ t ≤ 2π`.

**Central latitude α** is α₀, in [0, π/2]. **Flower amplitude A** is nonnegative and must keep the entire range α₀ ± A within [0, π/2]; invalid ranges are refused under the amplitude control rather than clipped into a different path. **Petals**, k, is a whole number from 1 to 8. Equality at either range boundary touches a pole and is supported. The gallery opens α₀ = π/4, A = π/8, k = 5, with 20 static samples and 128 arc samples. Choosing the flower from an edited latitude initially uses A = 0 and k = 5, retaining the latitude's α; previously edited flower values remain available when switching back.

**Sampling and identity.** The n **Fibers on the flower** (1–32) sample tⱼ = 2πj/n, without a duplicate endpoint sample. This is uniform parameter sampling, not uniform spherical arc length. Fiber subdivisions N remain 8–256. The base curve uses B = max(N, 16k) segments for A > 0, retaining at least 16 segments per petal even at the lowest fiber resolution; its endpoint is its first point exactly. A = 0 keeps the latitude's N segments. The same budget applies with B + 1 in place of N + 1 for the base curve; the largest admitted upper count is still 10,346 points. No frame geometry is retained for the length of a movie.

The existing source namespace `weave/latitude/0` is retained for stable sample identities: `/base/j` identifies the point and `/fiber/j` its fiber. A nonconstant flower's section has kind `flower`, dimension 1; it is a path, not a torus. Base samples within the existing 10⁻¹²-radian pole snap are the exact poles. Repeated samples at the same pole share one drawn point and fiber, identified by the first such sample. The optional correspondence `aliases` array maps every original sample index to its drawn representative; `selected` retains the entered sample index, and picking its shared drawing retains that selection. Distinct non-pole samples remain separate. `coincident` reports when the moving point lies at a pole shared by several sampled points.

**Tracing.** **Trace the flower** uses the same absolute parameter and paired sampler as the latitude. At a sampled parameter (within 8 × 10⁻¹⁵ turns of rounding), it highlights that frozen sample's existing fiber rather than drawing another. At a pole already sampled, it uses that pole's shared fiber. Otherwise it emits `weave/moving` and `weave/moving/base` and reports `movingSample = −1`. The charts are evaluated independently at each base point, never interpolated across their gauge seam. Changing chart shifts the circle's phase, not its geometry or connectivity. Analytic window classification remains unchanged after 4D rotation.

Trace and both rotation motions are offered. Latitude sweeping and its endpoints apply only to the latitude path; switching from a sweep to the flower selects Trace the flower and resets playback. Switching to the ordinary loom restores its controls and ignores flower parameters. The path, amplitude and petal count travel in links (`weave.path`, `weave.amplitude`, `weave.petals`); absent fields retain the original latitude, amplitude 0 and k = 5. The trace remains transient. Both cameras, appearance, stills and movies use the same paired composition described above.

**Checks.** Returned base geometry lies on S² and every unrotated returned fiber lies on S³ and maps to its own point. ## Scale and translation

Every tolerance in `engine4` is absolute plus relative to the study's own scale. What a study can move:

- **Tesseract and spherical weave:** neither. They are the unit tesseract and the unit 3-sphere, centered at the origin, and the request carries no size or center for them; the shared size fields leave every mode unchanged. The weave's window radius sets the window, not the sphere.
- **Ball and tube:** scale only, since they have no center. Radii, slice and spread scale together.
- **Shell bypass:** scale only, since the obstacle is centered at the origin. Radii, ε, the outside point, H and both comparison levels scale together.
- **Lift:** the sources are fixed curves, so the lift moves past them rather than scaling with them. Moving the center along the threads moves every interior cut and lifted point by the same amount. The missing radius scales with the support L at a fixed height, and lifted fourth coordinates scale with the height A. The slab half-thickness ε = 0.02 is a fixed modeling length, not a tolerance, so the lift is deliberately not scale-covariant in A.

**Budget.** A power-of-two scale reproduces every returned number exactly: points, parameters, section levels and radii, hits, clearance, distances and framing. Other scales agree within 10⁻¹² of the framing radius, and parameters within 10⁻¹². A study more than 10⁻⁸ from a threshold, relative to its own scale, keeps its outcome at every supported scale; one within 10⁻¹³ is coalesced at every supported scale.

| Tolerance | Value | Scale | Relative band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curved tangent slice, on h | 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ s | support s, 0.001–100 | 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ to 1.1 × 10⁻⁹ |
| Bypass sphere tangency, on r² − d² | 10⁻¹² (1 + r²) | radius r, 0.05–20 | δ ≤ 5 × 10⁻¹³ (1 + r⁻²): below 2 × 10⁻¹⁰ |
| Bypass contact at a cut, on radius and \|w\| | 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ b | outer radius b | 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ of b |
| Lift present set, on d² | 10⁻¹² r (r + √10) | missing radius r | δ ≤ 5 × 10⁻¹³ (1 + √10/r): below 4 × 10⁻¹¹ for r ≥ 0.05 |
| Lift line event | 10⁻¹² + 10⁻¹⁰ max(1, r²) | — | none: below zero residual it only repeats the closest-point event |
| Lift circle event, on a cosine; event coalescing, on a parameter | 10⁻¹²; 10⁻¹³ | dimensionless | — |
| Weave window and latitude snap, on q_w and radians | 10⁻¹² | unit sphere | — |
| Tesseract stereographic coefficients, cut spacing; section level, coincidence and planarity | 10⁻¹⁴; 10⁻¹⁰, 10⁻⁹ (10⁻¹⁸ squared), 10⁻⁹ | unit tesseract, coordinates within 2 | fixed object |

δ is the relative distance from the threshold that is coalesced. The absolute floor 10⁻¹² dominates the curved and bypass bands at small radii, so their bands widen from about 10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁹ across the supported range; both stay inside the budget. The shell's b − a ≥ 0.001 is a validation limit, not a tolerance, and is also absolute, so a thin shell can become invalid when scaled down.

**Checks.** `engine4/scale_test.go` covers the ball and tube tangent slices at supports 0.001–100, the radial shell touching H = b and the embedded route on the slab boundary H = ε at scales 1/16–8, and a thread tangent to the missing ball at missing radii 0.06–7.5. Making the curved, bypass or lift-membership tolerances purely absolute fails these tests, as does offsetting the missing radius by 10⁻⁹. The lift line-event tolerance is inert: replacing it with 10³ passes the whole suite.
