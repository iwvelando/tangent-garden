# Using Tangent Garden

## Explore

- **Browse all** on the example card opens a gallery of every preset, drawn as it first appears, grouped by family and searchable by title, description, or construction. The ‹ and › buttons beside the card step through presets in order.
- Forty-seven presets cover representative notebook studies, parallel illumination, pedal-family projections, a normal offset, an offset stack, the generating circles of an envelope, a rolling-circle roulette, a circle rolling on a curve, two curves rolling on curves, two families of circle chords, a family of folding creases, three families of moving circles, three inversions, two Lissajous figures, a Fourier curve of rotating circles, two cyclic pursuits, two vector fields, two implicit curves, three iterated maps, and four constructions on a derived curve.
- Input parametric `x(t), y(t)`, Cartesian `y=f(x)`, or polar `r(t)` curves, or generate a roulette, a Lissajous figure, a Fourier curve, a cyclic pursuit, the trajectories of a vector field, the level set of an implicit curve, or the visit density of an iterated map. Interval bounds and every numeric parameter field (shape parameter `a`, roulette and rolling-circle radii, tracing distances, and phases, Lissajous amplitudes, frequencies, and phase, Fourier term frequencies, radii, and phases, pursuers' starting coordinates and speeds and the capture distance, vector-field seeds and escape radius, an implicit curve's level, window, and family levels, an iterated map's coefficients, start, and window, a rolling curve's domain, start, and tracing point, pole and source coordinates, inversion center and radius, travel direction, refractive indices, offset and involute distances) accept constant expressions such as `2*pi`, `-phi`, `pi/2`, or `ln(e)` through the same Go parser. The field keeps the text you entered; an invalid entry is reported with the field's name. Whole-number counts (samples, construction lines, number of offsets, grid cells, level count, discarded and accumulated iterates) take plain numbers.
- Expressions support `+ - * / ^`, parentheses, `pi ≈ 3.1415926536`, `e ≈ 2.7182818285`, `phi ≈ 1.6180339887` (the positive golden ratio), and the functions shown in the UI. Multiplication is explicit. Exponentiation is right associative; `-t^2` means `-(t^2)`. This is a bounded parser, never JavaScript evaluation. An adjustable `a` is available in curve expressions; A vector field's expressions use `x`, `y`, and `t`; an implicit curve's `F` uses `x` and `y`. Bounds and animation endpoints must be constant, with no `t`, `x`, `y`, or `a`.
- **Definition → Roulette · rolling circle** traces a point on a circle that rolls without slipping. **Rolling** chooses **Inside a fixed circle** (a hypotrochoid), **Outside a fixed circle** (an epitrochoid), or **Along a line** (a trochoid). Set **Fixed radius R** (not used on a line), **Rolling radius r**, **Tracing distance d** from the rolling center, and **Phase φ** in radians, which turns the tracing arm counterclockwise from the contact point at t = 0. A circle rolling inside must be smaller than the fixed one. Here t is the angle of the rolling center around the fixed center, or the rolling circle's own turn on a line. d = r traces the rim with cusps; larger d gives loops. When R/r is a ratio of whole numbers p/q with q ≤ 200, a note reports that the trace closes after q turns with p arches, and **Trace one full period** sets the domain end to the start plus 2πq; other ratios are never forced closed. The fixed circle or line, the rolling circle at the end of the drawn trace, its center, arm, and contact point are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**, and framing always includes the fixed circle and every rolling position. Any construction tab applies to the roulette. Switching back to an expression format keeps your expressions. **Hypotrochoid & its evolute** is R = 5, r = 2, d = 3 over t from 0 to 4π, whose evolute is a five-pointed star.
- **Definition → Lissajous · A sin(mt + φ), B sin(nt)** traces x = A sin(mt + φ), y = B sin(nt). Set **Amplitude A** and **Amplitude B** (0–100,000), **Frequency m** and **Frequency n** (real, within ±1,000), and **Phase φ** in radians. Each coordinate is drawn as the projection of a point turning on a guide circle: the x guide above the figure and the y guide to its right, with their radii and dashed projection lines to the traced point. **Definition → Fourier · rotating circles** adds 1–16 rotating vectors, each a **Term** with **Frequency k**, **Radius r**, and **Phase φ** (its angle from +x at t = 0, in radians); positive frequencies turn counterclockwise. The vectors are chained from the origin in the order listed, each circle centered where the previous vector ends. **Add a term** appends one turning the other way at the next frequency and half the last radius; **Remove** deletes a term, keeping at least one. For both, t is time, and a note reports closure. When every frequency that moves the curve is a whole number, the curve repeats after t spans 2π divided by their greatest common divisor. Other whole-number ratios, with denominators up to 1,000, close after their common period. Other ratios are never forced closed. **Trace one full period** sets the domain end to the start plus the period, as a multiple of π where it is one. Terms of zero radius or amplitude do not affect closure. The guides, circles, vectors, and traced point are drawn where the trace ends and hide with **Construction lines**; framing includes every circle at every representative sample. Any construction tab applies, and switching back to an expression format keeps your expressions. **Lissajous 3 : 2 & its pedal** is x = sin(3t + π/2), y = sin 2t with its four-petalled pedal about the center. **Epicycles, turned inside out** is e^{it} + 0.45e^{−4it} + 0.2e^{6it}, a five-armed star, inverted in the unit circle into a flower. **Lissajous √2 : 1, never closing** fills its box without repeating, and so does its pedal.
- **Definition → Pursuit · each chases the next** sets 2–16 pursuers, each with **Start x**, **Start y** (its position when t is at the domain start, within ±100,000) and **Speed v** (0–100,000). Each runs straight at the next, the last at the first; t is time. **Add a pursuer** appends one halfway from the last back to the first at the last one's speed; **Remove** keeps at least two; **Space evenly on a circle** places them counterclockwise, in chase order, on the circle about their centroid through the farthest, starting from the first, keeping speeds. A pursuer's direction is undefined on its target, so the chase stops, for everyone, the first time any pursuer comes within **Capture distance ε** of its own target; later samples are left empty, and nobody merges or changes target. A note names the pair and time, and **End the domain at the capture** sets the domain end there. Every pursuer's path is drawn as a curve; the polygons connecting their positions at each representative sample and the pursuers at the end of the drawn chase are construction geometry and hide with **Construction lines**. Framing includes every path. Constructions apply to the first pursuer's path. **Seven pursuers & an evolute** starts from the unit heptagon at unit speed: the polygons stay regular as they turn and shrink, every path is a logarithmic spiral, and the evolute of the first is the same spiral scaled by tan(π/7). **Four chasers at unequal speeds** starts from a square with speeds 1, 1.25, 1, and 0.8; the square skews as it turns, and the third catches the slow fourth at t ≈ 1.96 while the others are still apart.
- **Definition → Vector field · trajectories** follows the field whose velocity at (x, y) at time t is (**dx/dt**, **dy/dt**). These are expressions in `x`, `y`, `t`, and `a`.
  - **Seeds:** set 1–16 seeds, each with **Seed x** and **Seed y** (its position when t is at the domain start, within ±100,000). **Add a seed** continues the line through the last two seeds by one more step, or puts the new seed half a unit to the right of a single one. **Remove** keeps at least one. Each trajectory follows the field on its own, and t is time.
  - **Ends:** a trajectory ends where it leaves the circle of **Escape radius R** about the origin (0–100,000), where the field stops being finite or changes too fast to follow, or where its integration step budget runs out. A seed outside the circle has no path. A note says where and why each trajectory that ended early did so.
  - **Drawing:** every trajectory is drawn as a curve, and constructions apply to the first. The escape circle (dashed), the seeds (rings), chevrons showing each trajectory's direction of travel at each representative sample, and the direction field are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**. The direction field is a grid of arrows spanning the trajectories. It is drawn only when neither expression mentions t, because a field that changes with t has no single direction field. Framing includes every trajectory, but not the escape circle or the grid.
  - **Van der Pol limit cycle** is ẋ = y, ẏ = a(1 − x²)y − x with a = 1. A trajectory from near the origin and one from far out wind onto the same closed orbit, with a stack of parallel curves winding with the first.
  - **Pendulum phase portrait** is ẋ = y, ẏ = −sin x. Three swings stay on closed orbits, with the evolute of the first. Four seeds above the separatrix go over the top and leave the escape circle, mirrored pairs at equal times.
- **Definition → Implicit · F(x, y) = c** draws the level set of **F(x, y)**, an expression in `x`, `y`, and `a`, where it equals **Level c**.
  - **Window:** the curve is sought only between **Window x from**/**to** and **Window y from**/**to** (within ±100,000). **Grid cells** (4–1,024) divides the window's longer side. F is sampled at the cells' corners, so a piece of the curve smaller than a cell can be missed; the crossings, and the curve between them, are then found exactly.
  - **No construction:** the curve has no parameter, so the construction tabs are disabled, and the domain and samples play no part. Switching back to another definition restores them.
  - **Family of levels** adds **Level count** (2–64) levels evenly spaced from **Levels from** to **Levels to**, both included, drawn in the derived colour.
  - **Drawing:** the curve is drawn in the base colour. The dashed window, arrows showing F's gradient at points evenly spaced along the curve (as many as the construction lines, pointing toward larger F), and small crosses where F changes sign without reaching a level are construction geometry; they hide with **Construction lines**. Where F jumps or has a pole, no contour is drawn across; where it is undefined, the cells beside it are left out. A level F only touches, at an isolated point or along a fold, is not drawn. A note counts the pieces, the discontinuities, and the undefined grid points. Framing shows the whole window.
  - **Cassini ovals & the lemniscate** draws the lemniscate ((x − a)² + y²)((x + a)² + y²) = 1 with a = 1, among a family of Cassini ovals: two apiece below it, one above. Animating **Level c** splits and joins the ovals at the saddle.
  - **Circles through two points** draws y/(x² + y² − a²) = c, circles through (±a, 0). F has a pole along the circle of radius a, marked with crosses, which no contour crosses.
- Point sources default to Cartesian x/y independently of curve format. Choose **Source coordinates → Polar** for nonnegative radius and theta in radians, counterclockwise from +x. Switching coordinates preserves the location. For parallel light, 0° travels right and 90° travels up.
- Refraction uses **incident index n₁ / transmitted index n₂**. Normals face each incident ray. Total internal reflection produces an amber reflected ray, with no transmitted envelope at that sample.
- **Ellipse & its pedal** projects an independent pole onto the tangent at each sampled point. Set **Pole x** and **Pole y** in Cartesian coordinates; the pole may lie on the curve. Each representative construction has a tangent segment and a perpendicular from the pole to its foot. Both follow the Construction lines toggle; the count selects representative constructions, each with two segments. A gold marker identifies the pole; these settings do not change the optical source.
- The **Pedal** tab holds the whole pole family. Its **Projection** selector chooses **Pedal** (foot on the tangent), **Contrapedal** (foot on the normal), or **Orthotomic** (the pole reflected across the tangent). Switching keeps the pole, and leaving and returning to the tab keeps the selection. **Ellipse & its contrapedal** projects the center onto each normal. **Ellipse & its orthotomic** reflects a focus across each tangent; the reflections land on a circle about the other focus. Orthotomic constructions draw the genuine foot like a pedal, then dash the reflected half from the foot to the orthotomic point.
- The **Offset** tab moves every point a signed **Offset distance d** along its normal. Positive distances go to the left of the direction of travel, inward on a counterclockwise closed curve; negative distances go to the right. Each construction line is a normal segment of length |d|. Once d exceeds a radius of curvature, the offset folds into cusps on the evolute and swallowtail loops; these are part of the curve, not errors. At a cusp of the curve itself the normal flips to the other side, so the offset jumps: it is left open there, with a numerical note, even between samples. The involute and a circle envelope's two branches, which also follow the direction of travel, are left open at cusps in the same way. **Flower & its offset** moves 0.4 inward from a five-petal polar curve whose tips curve more tightly than that. The offset distance is independent of the involute's string offset. **Offsets → A stack of offsets** instead draws **Number of offsets** (2–64) parallel curves evenly spaced from **First offset distance** to **Last offset distance**, both included; each construction line crosses the whole stack along a normal and reaches back to the curve. Offsets × samples may not exceed 131,072. **Generating circles** draws a circle at each construction line with the largest distance as its radius, |d| for one offset; the offsets at ±radius are their envelope. The circles are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**, but framing always keeps them in view. **Flower & its offset stack** reproduces eighteen offsets 0.105 apart; **Circles & their envelope** rolls a radius-0.5 coin around an ellipse. The single distance and the stack settings are remembered separately when switching.
- The **Rolling** tab rolls a circle without slipping along the curve, whatever its definition (or along the derived curve chosen under **Construct on**), and traces a point fixed to it. **Side of the curve** puts the circle on the **Left of travel** (inside a counterclockwise closed curve) or the **Right of travel**. Set **Circle radius ρ**, **Tracing distance ℓ** from the circle's center, and **Phase ψ** in radians, which turns the tracing arm counterclockwise from the contact at the domain start. ℓ = 0 traces a parallel curve, ℓ = ρ the rim with cusps on the curve, and larger ℓ loops. Each construction line joins a contact point to its tracing point and is normal to the traced curve. The circle, its center, arm, contact, and tracing point are drawn where the trace ends and hide with **Construction lines**. The circle starts rolling at the domain start and stops, with a numerical note, at an invalid sample. At a cusp, where the curve turns back on itself, it stays on its side of the curve and rolls back out the way a coin rolled into a V would, turning the other way; a note counts the cusps; it may overlap the curve where it is larger than the radius of curvature, as the mathematical roulette does. On a circle or line it matches the **Roulette** definition. **Flower & a rolling circle** rolls a rim point inside the five-petal flower, with ten circumferences nearly one lap, so its ten cusps touch the petal tips and waists.
- **Rolling shape → Curve · x(t), y(t)** rolls a second curve instead of a circle. Enter **Rolling x(t)** and **Rolling y(t)** in the rolling curve's own frame (they may use `a`), its domain **Rolling t from**/**to**, the point that first touches the base, **Contact starts at t**, and a **Tracing point** (x, y) in the same frame. Equal arc lengths are rolled on both curves, which stay tangent at the contact. On the **Left of travel** the contact runs toward the end of the rolling curve's domain and its own left side faces the base's left; on the **Right** it runs toward the start and its left side faces the base's right, so a counterclockwise closed curve rolls inside or outside as a circle does. A closed rolling curve wraps around indefinitely; an open one, or one with a cusp or undefined point, stops there with a numerical note. At a cusp of the curve it rolls along, a rolling curve rolls back out like the circle, so an open one can also run off its starting end. The rolling curve is drawn where the trace ends, with the contact, tracing point, and the line joining them, and hides with **Construction lines**. The circle's settings are kept while the curve is selected. **Ellipse rolling on an ellipse** rolls an ellipse around its twin from matching vertices: its focus traces a circle of radius 2a about the far focus. **Ellipse rolling in a flower** rolls an ellipse whose perimeter is a fifth of the flower's inside it, so its vertex returns to each petal tip and the trace closes a five-pointed star.
- The **Envelope** tab draws a family of lines or circles, one for each point of the curve, and the curve they all touch. **Family → Chords to a second point** joins the curve's point at t to **Second point x(t)**, **y(t)** at the same t (they may use `a`); each chord is drawn between its endpoints, and the second points are drawn as a thin curve with the base. Envelope points on a chord's extension, beyond the segment, are dashed and hide with **Virtual extensions**; **Extend chords to full lines** draws the full lines instead, with no virtual part. Where the two endpoints coincide the chord has no direction: it is omitted and the envelope has a gap, with a numerical note. **Family → Lines turned to an angle θ(t)** instead turns each line through the curve's point to **Direction angle θ(t)**, in radians counterclockwise from +x; these lines are unbounded and drawn across the whole view. A direction turning by exactly π is the same line. Where neighbouring lines are parallel they meet only at infinity and the envelope has a gap. **Chords & a cardioid** joins angle t to angle 2t on the unit circle with x = cos(a·t), y = sin(a·t) and a = 2; its chords envelope a cardioid. **Chords of four** is the 200-chord reference study, t to 4t, whose envelope has three cusps. **Folding a parabola** creases a sheet so the point (0, 1) lands on each point of the x-axis; the creases, at angle atan(x), envelope the parabola x² = 4y. **Family → Circles of radius R(t)** centers a circle of **Circle radius R(t)** (it may use `a`) on each point of the curve, drawn with the construction lines along with its radii to the points where it touches the envelope. The envelope has two branches, to the left and right of travel, drawn in the derived colour. They merge where the radius changes exactly as fast as the center moves (|R′| equals the curve's speed), and vanish where it changes faster: the circles nest, and a note counts the gaps. A branch that collapses to one point is drawn as a dot. Where the radius is not positive there is no circle, and a stationary center has no envelope point; both are counted in notes. A constant radius gives the offsets ±R. **Circles through a focus** centers circles on a parabola so that each passes through its focus: they touch the directrix, and their other branch is the focus itself. **Breathing circles** swells and shrinks circles around a circle with radius 1 + 0.35 sin(a·t). **Swelling circles** grows its radius faster than its center moves in places, so its branches break into pieces.
- The **Inversion** tab inverts a curve in a circle: each point p moves along its ray from the **Inversion center** (x, y) to the point whose distance from the center, times p's, is the square of **Inversion radius R**. The curve inverted is the one chosen under **Construct on**: the curve itself, whatever its definition (roulettes included), or one of its derived curves. The image is the derived curve. The dashed circle of inversion and the segments joining points to their images, along rays from the center, are construction lines; the center is marked. Where the inverted curve passes through the center, its image runs off to infinity and is left open there, never joined across, with a numerical note; a pass close to the center between samples is found too, and more samples resolve it. A point exactly on the center has no image. Where the curve runs off to infinity, its image passes through the center. **Hyperbola into a lemniscate** inverts x² − y² = 1 about its center into Bernoulli's lemniscate. **Hypotrochoid, turned inside out** inverts a roulette, so the loops nearest the center become the outer petals. **An ellipse's pedal, inverted** inverts the pedal about its pole into the ellipse's polar reciprocal, another ellipse.
- The involute is `r − (s+c)T`, with `s=0` at the interval's lower bound and adjustable initial offset `c`.
- **Construct on**, at the end of the curve's settings, chooses the curve every construction acts on: **The curve itself**, or one of its derived curves, **Its evolute**, **Its pedal**, **Its contrapedal**, **Its orthotomic**, or **Its offset**. A pedal-family input uses **Pole x** and **Pole y**, and an offset input uses **Offset distance d**; they appear beside it when the construction does not show them itself, and they are the same pole and distance the construction uses, so a pedal of a pedal has one pole. The derived curve is drawn faintly with the curve, its segments start on it, and the numerical notes and gaps are the construction's. The choice stays as you change tabs, except that the evolute cannot feed the evolute or the caustics (the option is disabled there, and moving to them returns to the curve itself): those would need the curve's fourth derivative. Formats with no parameter have nothing to construct on. **Cayley's sextic, a second pedal** takes the pedal of a circle about a point on it, a cardioid, and then the cardioid's pedal about its cusp. **Unwinding an evolute** unwinds a string from a logarithmic spiral's evolute, starting as long as the radius of curvature at t = 0, and retraces the spiral. **Rolling on an ellipse's pedal** rolls a circle 14 times around the pedal of an ellipse about its center. **Offsets of an evolute** draws nine parallels of an ellipse's evolute; each is left open at the evolute's cusps, where it jumps to the other side.
- Toggle construction lines, incident rays, dashed backward extensions, curves, and axes. Drag to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom (about the point between your fingers), or fit the view. Light/dark backgrounds and image export preserve the visible drawing. **Export image** in the header opens a menu: **PNG image** (2000 × 1520 unless you choose another size, drawn from the vectors at that size, so it is sharp and works almost anywhere) or **SVG** (vector, scalable; its metadata includes the mathematical inputs). Below them, **PNG size** offers 1000 × 760, 2000 × 1520, 3000 × 2280 or 4000 × 3040, and **Transparent background** leaves the paper out of both formats, so the drawing can be laid over anything; its lines keep the theme's colors. These settings keep the menu open and stay chosen for the next export. A size the browser cannot draw (iOS Safari limits canvas area) is refused with a message naming it, and nothing is saved. An iterated map's density is a raster of its cells: SVG embeds it as a PNG with one pixel per cell, and the menu says so; its window and dots stay vectors. PNG and animation exports draw those cells at the target size, square-edged, like the rest of the drawing. Both capture exactly what is shown, including pan/zoom and a paused animation frame.
- **Line weight**, after the layers, draws every line **Fine**, **Regular** (the drawing as it always was), or **Bold**, as a share of the drawing so stills and videos keep the proportion, or as a one-pixel **Hairline**, which grows fainter in a larger export. The 4D notebook offers the same choice after its layers, and the 3D notebook under **Lines**. Links carry it; older links open with regular strokes.
- **Refine between samples**, under the samples, adds points between the evenly spaced samples wherever the line drawn between two of them strays from the curve by more than 1/5000 of the curve's size, at most 10 halvings of a sample step and 16,384 added points per curve, and the readout below says what it added, broke, or left unresolved. It refines the curve, a derived curve it is built on, and a pedal, contrapedal, orthotomic, evolute, offset, caustic, inverted curve, involute, rolling trace or envelope, with each curve of an offset stack and each branch of a circle envelope refined on its own; every construction line, circle and rolling position keeps the samples. The solid real part and dashed virtual part of a caustic, or of a chord envelope, are drawn to meet where they change. It is not offered for a pursuit, a vector field, a level set or an iterated map. **Ripples hidden between samples** (a coil the samples alias into a slow wave), **Petals that miss the center** (loops thrown out by inversion, round only when refined), **Ripples around a five-pointed star** (parallel curves swinging round nearly sharp points in half circles) **A wavefront through a three-pointed star** (one parallel curve moving from inside the star to outside it), **Sunlight in a hammered cup** (ripples too shallow to see in the wall break the cup's caustic into a starburst of cusps) **A crown of cusps in a sunlit cup** (finer ripples whose crown of cusps folds into the nephroid as they settle, and returns), **A string unwound from a three-pointed star** (a spiral built of the wide arcs the string's end sweeps at each nearly sharp point) **A string wound back onto a four-pointed star** (the string shortened by a lap, its cusp running round the points), **A coin rolled round a three-pointed star** (a round petal swept wherever the coin pivots on a point) and **Petals circling a four-pointed star** (the coin's phase turned once, so its petals and loops run round the star), **Circles swelling at a five-pointed star's points** (an envelope rounding each point along the circle there) and **Circles breathing round a three-pointed star** (the circles' radii swapping between the points and the sides) open refined.
- **Probe → Tangent, normal & curvature at a point** describes the base curve at one sample, chosen with **Point**: its tangent T and normal N, its osculating circle and the circle's center, and the point's construction lines, with the signed curvature κ (positive turning left), the radius 1/|κ| and the arc length s from the first sample, and κ plotted along the curve. Moving the probe never recomputes. Flat samples, centers beyond 100 radii of the study, and samples whose second derivative is unstable are counted beneath. It is not offered for a level set or an iterated map. **A clover's curvature, probed** opens with the probe moving along a curve that bends both ways.
- **Numerical samples** accepts any whole number from 64–32,768 and **Construction lines** any whole number from 2–2,048, with no more lines than samples. These workload limits apply to animations too. They are application guardrails, not intrinsic browser limits: computation and serialization grow with sample count, while SVG drawing grows with line count. Responsiveness depends on hardware, expressions, and construction. More samples improve spatial coverage but do not automatically improve derivative precision.
- The theme initially follows your system, including live time-of-day changes. Clicking the toggle saves an explicit light/dark preference in this browser. **Follow system** clears that override. Storage failures do not prevent using the app.
- On desktop, the parameter sidebar scrolls independently of the drawing. On narrow screens, the drawing and controls stack in the document.
- **Definition → Attractor · iterated map** counts where the orbit of an iterated map lands. **Map** chooses Clifford, Peter de Jong, or Hénon and loads its curated coefficients; the formula appears below the drawing's explanation.
  - **Coefficient a**–**d** (Hénon's map uses only a and b) and **Start x₀**/**y₀** define the orbit. **Discarded iterates** (0–1,000,000) are dropped while the orbit settles; the next **Accumulated iterates** (0–5,000,000) are counted in **Grid cells** (4–1,024) along the window's longer side.
  - **Fit the window to the iterates** (on by default) frames the accumulated iterates exactly. Unchecked, **Window x from**/**to** and **Window y from**/**to** fix it, and iterates outside are counted in the note but not drawn.
  - **Drawing:** each cell is one pixel of the density, shaded by the logarithm of its visits and drawn with square edges at any size. The iterates are never joined. The dashed window, the start (a ring), and the first iterates from the start (dots, as many as the construction lines) are construction geometry. A note gives the iterates, the cells visited, the busiest cell, iterates outside the window, and where an orbit left |x|, |y| ≤ 100,000. There is no construction, domain, or sample count, as for implicit curves.
  - **Clifford attractor** is the reference study: (a, b, c, d) = (−1.4, 1.6, 1, 0.7) from (0.1, 0.1), 1,000 iterates discarded and 800,000 counted. **De Jong attractor** uses (1.4, −2.3, 2.4, −2.1); **Hénon map** uses (a, b) = (1.4, 0.3) from the origin. These are pictures of iterated maps, not proofs of chaos, and a different device or build can round differently and follow a different orbit with a similar density.
- The expandable diacaustic explanation covers index conventions, Snell's law, total internal reflection, and the supported inclusive range 0.01–10. Every finite decimal in that range is allowed; there is no 0.05-step restriction.

## Animate

Open **Animation** in the sidebar. Set a duration (0.1–3,600 seconds) and choose a mode:

**Draw along the curve** reveals the study from the domain minimum to maximum. For an iterated map it is **Accumulate the iterates**: each frame counts the first iterates, from none to all, in the finished drawing's window and grid. It reveals the already-computed base curve, derived curve, and representative lines in parameter order. The numerical grid and arc-length anchor stay fixed; this avoids changing the involute as the drawing grows. At the start, a zero-length trace is represented by its first point, not by an invalid zero-width computation domain.

**Vary parameters** adds one or more simultaneous linear tracks with **From** and **To** values. Every numeric simulation control is available: domain start/end, point-source x/y or polar radius/theta, pedal-family pole x/y, inversion center x/y and radius, a derived input's pole or offset distance, offset distance or the first/last distance and number of offsets in a stack, roulette radii, tracing distance, and phase, Lissajous phase, frequencies, and amplitudes, each Fourier term's phase, radius, and frequency, each pursuer's speed and starting x and y and the capture distance, each vector-field seed's x and y and the escape radius, an implicit curve's level, window edges, first and last family levels and level count, and grid cells, an iterated map's coefficients, start, discarded and accumulated iterates, window edges (when not fitted), and grid cells, rolling-circle radius, tracing distance, and phase, rolling-curve tracing point and contact start, the chord multiplier and the circles' lobes through `a`, parallel travel direction, incident/transmitted index, involute offset, sample count, line count, and shape parameter `a` (not for roulettes, harmonic curves, pursuits, or iterated maps, which do not use it; a vector field keeps it for its expressions). Ray length is also animatable. Only parameters applicable to the selected construction/source are offered. Counts are rounded to whole numbers; invalid endpoints are rejected with their start/end context, and a calculation error stops playback. Moving the domain start to its end creates a zero-width interval; derivative-based constructions need a positive width of at least 0.000001. Singular samples remain numerical gaps rather than stopping a valid study.

**Move the probe along the curve** (while the probe is on) moves it from the curve's first sample to its last over the fixed study, with its circle, frame and readout. With the probe on, **Vary parameters** also draws it in every frame, where **Probe** keeps it at its t (absent from a frame whose domain leaves that t out), at its share of the drawn length, or moves it along the curve as the parameters vary.

On a computer with spare cores, playback of calculated frames (parameter tracks, an iterated map's accumulation, and every 4D motion) shares the work with a second engine while it plays, so slow studies skip fewer frames.

**Trace rays** (catacaustics and diacaustics only) starts from the curve and its light alone. Light leaves a point source, or streams in from past the edge of the view for parallel light, strikes the curve, and reflects or refracts; each caustic point is drawn in the caustic's color as its ray reaches it, and each ray continues to its full ray length. Dashed extensions grow behind the curve at the same pace, and a virtual caustic point appears when its extension reaches it. Light slows to c/n in each medium, so refracted rays travel at n₁/n₂ of their incident speed and every wavefront stays together: a mirror that focuses parallel light brings all its rays to the focus at once. The timeline ends when every ray has its full length and every caustic point in view is reached; points beyond the view appear with the final frame. Only **Hold final view** and **Hold current view** are offered, since the light enters a fixed view.

Vary parameters → Tracing point x from `0.3` to `0` moves the tracing point from the vertex to the ellipse's center, opening the star's cusps into a smooth rosette; Contact starts at t from `0` to `pi/2` starts the roll on the ellipse's flank instead.

Vary parameters → Tracing distance ℓ from `0` to `0.3` grows the trace from a parallel curve through the rim's cusps into loops; Phase ψ from `0` to `2*pi` slides the cusps once around each arch.
- **Chords & a cardioid** → Vary parameters → Shape parameter a from `2` to `10` runs through the multiplication tables: each whole number m draws an epicycloid with m − 1 cusps, and the chords between them rearrange. Draw along the curve adds the chords one phase at a time.
- **Breathing circles** → Vary parameters → Shape parameter a from `5` to `8`: the circles gain lobes, and once |R′| outruns the center's speed, past a = 40/7, the two branches pinch together and break into pieces.

- **Unwinding a circle** → Draw along the curve → 10 seconds → Hold final view.
- **Light inside a circle** → Vary parameters → Source x from `1` to `.75` → 30 seconds. Add Source y for a moving source in both coordinates.
- For an orbit: set a polar curve `r(t)=1`, domain `0` to `2*pi`, and a polar point source with radius `1` and theta `0`. Animate **Source theta θ (radians)** from `0` to `pi/2`. The source follows a quarter circle; radius may have a simultaneous track. Polar sources also work with Cartesian and parametric curves.
- **Parallel light & a circle** → Trace rays: the rays sweep in from the left edge, and the nephroid grows from its cusp as the reflected light crosses it.
- **Parallel light & a circle** → Vary parameters → Travel direction from `0` to `360` → 30 seconds. Angles here are degrees, and interpolation follows the entered values without shortest-path wrapping.
- Write `a*cos(t)` and `a*sin(t)` and animate `a` from `1` to `phi`. More generally, expressions such as `(1-a)*cos(t)+a*cos(2*t)` allow shape transitions; categorical choices such as construction type and curve format are held fixed during a run.

Four camera choices are available:

| Camera                    | Behavior                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hold final view           | Fit the final result once and retain its center and scale throughout.                                                                           |
| Hold current view         | Capture your current manual pan/zoom for playback and export.                                                                                   |
| Follow center, fixed zoom | Use the final scale, but center on the current geometry. Parameter animations that grow larger than the final extent can still leave the frame. |
| Fit each frame            | Fit the current geometry on every displayed frame.                                                                                              |

Runs use a clean automatic camera except when **Hold current view** captures your manual framing. **Pause** freezes the current frame; the timeline then scrubs by dragging, clicking, or with the arrow, Home, and End keys, and image export (PNG or SVG) captures that frame; SVG also records its animation metadata. **Resume** continues for the remaining duration; **Replay** restarts. **Stop** restores the original study and manual pan/zoom. After completion, the final frame stays and the camera is released: pan and zoom (or orbit, in 3D and 4D) start from the animation's own framing, and Fit view (Reset view in 3D and 4D) returns to it. Scrubbing holds the animation camera again. Controls unlock and the button becomes **Back to study**, which restores the study and the manual view from before the animation; editing settings clears the completed preview for another run. Changing a simulation input also cancels the animation. Playback pauses when the tab becomes hidden.

Playback targets at most 30 displayed frames per second, using elapsed time rather than a fixed number of steps. A longer duration gives smaller parameter changes per frame. Only one live frame calculation is in flight per engine; slower devices skip intermediate times instead of accumulating a backlog. In the 3D notebook, parameter playback on a device with more than two cores adds a second engine while it plays, so one frame is calculated while the other is drawn; a large surface animates at nearly twice the frame rate. Endpoint preparation is outside the requested playback duration, and the last frame is always the exact endpoint.

In every notebook, **Repeat** plays an animation **Once**, as a **Loop** whose end joins its start, or **Back and forth** within the duration, and **Pace** keeps it **Steady** or eases it in and out on a half cosine. A loop plays only when its last frame is its first; Play compares the drawing at both ends (and in 3D the probe and the camera) and names what differs. In 2D only parameter tracks can loop, for example `a` from `0` to `2*pi` in an expression with `cos(t + a)`; drawing along the curve and tracing light offer back and forth. **A lamp circling a mirror** loops a lamp once around inside an elliptical mirror. Repeating playback continues until paused or stopped; scrubbing to the end pauses there, at the start again, rather than completing. Choosing an example brings its own repeat and pace, or plays once, steadily. See [mathematics](mathematics.md#seamless-loops) and, for 3D, [the spatial study](spatial-study.md#looping-and-back-and-forth).

**Export format** chooses between **MP4 video** (H.264; small files that play on phones, in messaging and social apps, and in browsers) and **animated WebP** (can loop; plays in browsers). MP4 files are typically far smaller at similar quality. The page offers only the formats this browser can encode, and the picker appears only when both work. Safari and every iOS browser cannot encode WebP, so they offer MP4 alone. MP4 is the default when available. MP4 files have no loop setting, so the loop checkbox applies to WebP only; video players decide whether to loop. If the browser's video encoder refuses the chosen resolution, the export button explains that instead of switching formats silently.

The export button (**Export animated WebP** or **Export MP4 video**) saves the entire configured animation, including both endpoints (a repeating animation leaves out its end, which is its start again, and its WebP always loops), with independent **Export resolution** and **Export quality** sliders. Resolution ranges from **500 × 380 to 2000 × 1520** in 250 × 190 increments; quality ranges from **1 to 100**. Defaults are **2000 × 1520**, with quality **60 for MP4** and **85 for WebP**; lossy WebP needs the higher setting to look clean, while MP4 at 60 is hard to tell from 95 at a fraction of the size. Each format keeps its own quality, so switching formats and back preserves your setting. The reset button restores the resolution and the current format's quality default. Resolution controls the actual SVG rasterization dimensions, not an upscale of a finished image. Quality controls compression independently (for MP4 it sets the maximum bitrate; smooth line art often encodes well below it, so MP4 size may change little): lower values make smaller files at the cost of detail. The larger default matters: at 1000 × 760 construction lines are about one pixel wide, and video and lossy WebP store colour at half resolution, so thin lines lose their colour and soften. Choose a smaller size for smaller files and faster export, and raise quality only if you see artifacts; quality 100 remains available. Twice the width and height means four times the pixels, and maximum encoding quality can cause a large additional jump in file size. Size is not proportional to the quality slider; it depends on the drawing and browser. Maximum quality is not a cross-browser lossless guarantee. Choose 15, 30 or 60 fps; the default is 30. Animated WebP stores every frame whole, so at 60 fps a WebP is about twice the size of the same export at 30, and the export settings say so; long or high-resolution exports may reach the 256 MiB limit. A WebP can optionally loop; the default plays once and holds its final frame. The export snapshots the theme and visible layers and uses the selected animation camera, including manual framing for **Hold current view**, independently of the current playback position. Frames are rendered individually with the same SVG renderer and encoded at the selected quality in the browser. Rendering may take longer than playback, but the saved duration is preserved to the nearest millisecond. Frame delays divide that duration evenly (within 1 ms); endpoints are included on the first and last frames.

Export shows progress and can be canceled. Parameter-animation exports calculate several frames at once on multi-core devices (at most two at a time on phones and tablets, to limit memory) and discard those extra calculations on cancel. Editing a simulation input also cancels it without saving a partial file. Limits are 7,200 frames (two minutes at 60 fps, four at 30, or eight at 15) and 256 MiB of compressed output. A browser that can encode neither format disables the export button and says so; single frames can still be saved as SVG. No file is ever mislabeled as a format it is not. Everything stays local, and static hosting remains sufficient.

This is a **mathematical construction explorer**, not a scene renderer. Every regular sampled point participates; there is no occlusion, solid-medium topology, or multiple-bounce tracing. A closed curve can therefore display ray families that would not all be illuminated in a physical object.

## Share a study

**Copy link** in the header of every notebook (a chain icon on the narrowest screens) copies a link that reopens the current study for anyone who follows it. The link carries:

- the whole definition: expressions, every parameter, sampling, and the construction, including values not shown by the current definition;
- the visible layers;
- the manual camera (2D pan and zoom; 3D and 4D orbit, pan, and zoom, including the coordinate diagram's view beside a 4D shadow);
- the animation setup: what is animated, its camera mode, duration, and parameter tracks with their endpoints as entered, and in the 3D notebook its camera path and whether the animation camera flies it, and the ray it rides. The 4D notebook carries its motion and duration.
- In 2D, the interval bounds as entered (such as `2*pi`), the ray length, the remembered pole construction, whether the probe is on and where it stands, and how it moves while parameters vary.
- In every notebook, the line weight.
- In 3D, whether the parameter probe is on, whether it describes the curve, the surface, or a mirror's light or the mirror itself, and where it stands; and the cut plane, whether it is on, its normal and offset, what it cuts, and whether its edge is drawn.

It leaves out your theme, which stays each viewer's own, the playback position, and export settings. A numeric field reopens with its exact value, written as a whole multiple or simple fraction of π where it is one, as for examples. Another constant expression reopens as a decimal (`2*e` becomes `5.43656365691809`), which draws the same study. Framing follows the drawing: the 2D view is exact, while a 3D or 4D view keeps its angles and zoom and adapts to the viewer's window shape.

The study travels in the part of the address after `#`, which browsers never send to a server; no study is uploaded or stored anywhere. A typical link is 1–2 KB. A study whose link would exceed 32 KB, which only very long expressions reach, is refused with a message. Opening a link replaces the notebook's study as choosing an example does, then removes it from the address bar, so later edits never sit under the original link. Copy is unavailable while the study has an error or is still resolving. Where the browser refuses clipboard access, the link appears selected for copying by hand.

A damaged or edited link leaves the current study unchanged and names the field it could not use. A link made by a newer version of the site asks you to reload. Links made before a field existed keep opening with that field's default.

## 3D curves

The header’s study dropdown keeps both edited studies on the same page. Spatial studies offer tangent ribbons, involute filaments, tangent-foot projections, tangent-line orthotomics, sphere inversions, framed ribbons, ruled surfaces, and tubes and canal surfaces of torus knots, custom `x(t), y(t), z(t)` expressions, harmonic sums, and vector-field trajectories, or show the curve alone. Start with one of ninety-one presets, then edit expressions and scalar parameters. Drag to orbit, shift-drag to pan, scroll to zoom, or use the drawing's keyboard controls; on a touch screen, pinch to zoom and move two fingers together to pan. Surface, tangent lines and boundary curves can be shown independently. Choosing an example sets its own layers, or shows every layer; try **The whole focal surface of an ellipsoid**, **A cage of rings round a (3, 4) knot** or **A Klein bottle in twenty-four slices**. Choose **Construction · Involute** to unwind taut strings from the curve instead: set the anchor t₀ where arc length starts and the string length c, or enable **Family of involutes** for 2–24 evenly spaced lengths. Filaments and strings are separate layers, and the anchor, c, family range and count are animation tracks.

Choose **Tangent-foot projection** to drop perpendiculars from an independent pole P onto the moving tangent lines, or **Tangent-line orthotomic** to rotate P half a turn around each tangent. **Pole x**, **Pole y**, and **Pole z** accept constants such as `pi` and can be animated together. The derived curve, **Perpendiculars & tangent feet**, and **Pole marker** have independent visibility controls. Try **A knot through perpendiculars** or **Half-turns around a helix**; under Animation, vary **Pole z** from `-pi` to `pi` to move the pole through the study. Small crosses mark the tangent feet and a larger cross marks P. A straight line gives a point image, retained as a cross.

Choose **Spatial definition · Harmonic sum** to build a curve from turning vectors: r(t) = c₀ + Σ[Aₖ cos(ωₖt) + Bₖ sin(ωₖt)]. Set the center **c₀**, and for each of up to eight terms its **Frequency ωₖ** and the x, y, z coordinates of **Aₖ** and **Bₖ**; **Add a term** and **Remove** renumber the fields. The domain **t from**/**to** decides closure: a note says whether the frequencies repeat and whether the domain spans whole periods, and **Trace one full period** sets it when they do but it does not. Incommensurate frequencies are drawn as an open arc, never forced closed. **Vector sums** and **Generating ellipses** are separate layers under any construction, and c₀, every term's frequency and coordinates, and the domain are animation tracks. Try **A harmonic trefoil**, **Tilted ellipses · 1 : 3 : −5**, or **An orbit that never closes**.

Under **Sampling & definition**, **Refine between samples** draws a curve given by a formula more finely wherever a chord between its evenly spaced samples strays from it: by more than 1/5000 of the curve's size, judged at three points along the chord, halving each sample step at most 10 times and adding at most 16,384 points per curve. The curve, a tangent projection, an inverted curve, each involute, each offset strand, a ruled surface's partner thread, a canal's meridians and the base under a derived input are refined; the ribbon, contact circles, vector sums and every surface keep the evenly spaced samples, and so do the framing, reveal and probe. A gap or jump found between samples breaks the curve and any surface built on it there, and with an inversion it decides each passage through the center. A readout under the checkbox counts the points added and anything left unresolved. It is not offered for vector fields and chases, which are integrated with their own steps, nor for surfaces, mirrors and level sets. Try **A coil hidden between samples**, **A rose that misses the center**, **Strings swung round a three-cornered hat**, **A string swept across a four-cornered crown**, **A six-stranded rope round a trefoil**, **Four strands wound round a cinquefoil**, **A beaded trefoil wound with spiral stripes** and **A cord twisted round a spring**, unchecking the box to compare.

For the developable, involute, framed ribbon, ruled surface and tube, **Built on** chooses the curve the construction acts on: the base curve, or its tangent-foot curve or tangent-line orthotomic from the pole, whose **Pole x**, **Pole y** and **Pole z** then appear and are animation tracks. The base curve, the perpendiculars to its tangents and the pole are drawn in grey as separate layers, the legend and probe describe the derived curve, and where the derived curve stops in a cusp (as it does at a pole on the curve) the construction is broken and the note counts the cusps. Try **Filaments off a cusp**, **A horn of perpendicular feet** or **Chords across a knot's reflection**, then move the pole. **Its involute** builds on the string's free end instead, unwound from **Input anchor t₀** with length **Input string c**, which are animation tracks and replace the pole; grey **Strings from the base** run from the base along its tangents to the involute. Choosing it keeps an anchor inside the domain and otherwise moves it to the domain's middle. Arc length never crosses a break of the base, and the note counts the samples beyond its reach. Try **Where a helix's tangents land**, **A trefoil's string, unwound** or **A sheet of Viviani's normals**.

Choose **Sphere inversion** to invert the curve in a sphere of center O and radius R, or set **Curve to invert** to a tangent projection from the pole. **Center x**, **Center y**, **Center z**, and **Sphere radius R** accept constants and are animation tracks. The image, the correspondence segments to it, and the sphere's great circles are separate layers. Try **A staircase drawn into a sphere**, **A trefoil turned inside out**, or **Inverted perpendiculars**, then animate the radius or move the center. A curve through the center opens out through infinity; the image is left unjoined there, and the explanation counts each passage.

Choose **Framed ribbon · offset strands** to carry a frame along the curve and hang a ribbon and offset strands from it. **Frame** is **Rotation-minimizing (transported)**, carried without turning about the tangent, or **Frenet (diagnostic)**, which follows the curvature and breaks where the curve is straight or inflects. For the transported frame, **N₀ x**, **N₀ y**, and **N₀ z** give the reference normal that starts it. **Angle θ₀** (radians) and **Twist (turns)** set the offset direction D and how many times it turns over the curve, **Half-width w** sets the ribbon, and **Offset d** and **Offset strands** (0–12) set the strands. On a closed loop, **Closed-loop seam** either shows where the carried frame returns turned or spreads the opposite twist along the loop; the note below says which, and by how many degrees. **Ribbon surface**, **Cross-lines**, **Ribbon edges**, **Strands**, **Frames**, and, on a closed loop, **Seam** are separate layers, and every field is an animation track. Try **A band around the trefoil**, **Strands braided on a helix**, **The seam of a carried frame**, **A six-stranded rope round a trefoil**, or **Four strands wound round a cinquefoil**.

Choose **Ruled surface · chords & threads** to join each point a(t) to a partner by a straight ruling. **Partner** is **The curve itself (chords)** or **A second thread b(t)**, entered as **b x(t)**, **b y(t)**, and **b z(t)** in t. The partner of t sits at parameter mt + δ, set by **Shift δ** and **Rate m**. On a closed curve, a whole-number rate keeps the surface closed. On an open curve, chords that would leave the domain are dropped and counted. The note says whether the surface closes, where it stops or pinches, and whether it is developable. **Ruled surface**, **Rulings**, and **Partner thread** are separate layers: hide the surface for a drawing of threads alone. Shift and rate are animation tracks. Try **A harmonic loom**, **Chords across the trefoil**, or **Chords of a rising helix**, then animate **Shift δ** to turn one thread against the other.

Choose **Tube · canal surface** to roll a sphere along the curve and draw the surface that touches every sphere. Each sphere has radius **Tube radius R** times **Profile ρ(t)**, an expression in t; a profile of 1 gives a tube. **Meridians** (0–12) draws curves at evenly spaced angles around the surface. N₀, **Angle θ₀**, **Twist (turns)**, and **Closed-loop seam** carry that angle as for a framed ribbon; they move the meridians, not the surface. The note says whether the surface closes, where the radius changes too fast for an envelope, where a circle collapses to a point, and where the surface folds through itself. **Canal surface**, **Contact circles**, **Meridians**, **Frames**, and, on a closed loop, **Seam** are separate layers. Tube radius R, the angle, twist, meridians and N₀ are animation tracks. Try **A tube around the trefoil**, **A necklace of spheres**, or **Beads that lose their envelope**, then animate **Tube radius R**.

Choose **Spatial definition · Vector field** to follow a field in space: enter **dx/dt**, **dy/dt**, and **dz/dt** in x, y, z, t and a, the **Shape parameter a**, up to twelve seeds (**Seed x₁**, **Seed y₁**, **Seed z₁**, …; **Add a seed** and **Remove** renumber them), the time interval **t from**/**to**, and the **Escape radius R** of a sphere about the origin. Every trajectory runs from its seed at the interval start, integrated with its own adaptive steps, and stops early where it leaves the sphere, where the field stops being finite, or when its steps run out. The note says which and when, names seeds at rest on an equilibrium, and says whether the field changes with t. The first seed's trajectory is the curve every construction builds on; choose **Construction · None · the curve alone** to show the trajectories by themselves. **Other trajectories**, **Field directions** (strokes of one length showing direction, not speed), and **Seeds & early stops** are separate layers. a, every seed coordinate, the escape radius, and the interval are animation tracks. Try **A rising vortex**, **Lorenz's two wings**, or **A ribbon along Rössler's band**, then animate a with Lorenz's equations or move a seed.

Choose **Spatial definition · Pursuit** for a cyclic chase in space: 2–16 pursuers, each with a start (**Start x₁**, **Start y₁**, **Start z₁**, …) and a **Speed v₁**, runs straight at the next, the last at the first (**Add a pursuer** and **Remove** renumber them). Set the interval **t from**/**to** and the **Capture distance ε**: the chase stops for everyone the first time any pursuer comes that close to its own target, and nobody merges or changes target. The note names the capture and its time, or says the step budget ran out or that nobody was caught; **End the interval at the capture** trims the empty tail. The first pursuer's path is the curve every construction builds on; **Other pursuers**, **Connecting polygons**, and **Starts & capture** are separate layers. Every start, speed, the capture distance, and the interval are animation tracks. Try **Four pursuers on a tetrahedron**, **A chase untangling a trefoil**, or **A crown of six with a tangent ribbon**.

Choose **Spatial definition · Surface patch** to study a surface rather than a curve; the construction controls give way to its own. Pick a **Surface** (an ellipsoid or sphere, a torus, an elliptic cylinder, a paraboloid or saddle, or a monkey saddle). Choosing a kind starts from its own shape and whole chart; set its shape fields (for example **Axis a**, **Major radius R**, **Curvature k₁**) and the parameter domain **u from**/**u to** and **v from**/**v to**. A domain shorter than the whole chart opens the surface so that what lies inside shows. **Normal** points n along X_u × X_v or reverses it. **Offset d** draws the parallel surface X + dn (0 hides it), and **Normal reach ℓ** draws normal lines from X to X + ℓn, inwards when negative, at the crossings of the parameter curves. The note says whether each focal sheet is a surface, collapses to a curve or a point, or lies at infinity, and counts chart singularities, umbilics, and offset samples beyond a focal sheet. **Surface patch**, **Parameter curves**, **Normal lines**, **Offset surface**, **Focal sheet 1 · κ₁** (rust) and **Focal sheet 2 · κ₂** (slate) are separate layers. **Sampling & definition** sets **u samples** and **v samples** (12–240 each, at most 14,400 cells) and **Parameter curves** (2–48 each way). The shape, offset, reach, domain and grid are animation tracks, and a reveal grows the surface along u. Try **A torus revealing its centers**, **A sphere collapsing to one focus**, **The focal sheets of an ellipsoid**, or **An elliptic cylinder and its evolute**, then animate the offset.

Choose **Spatial definition · Mirror or interface · rays** to light one of those surfaces as a mirror and follow a single reflection. The surface, shape, and domain controls are shared with the surface study; **Mirror side** says which way the mirror faces, and light arriving from behind it, or grazing it, is unlit. **Light** is **Parallel light**, travelling at **Azimuth α (°)** and **Elevation β (°)** (β = −90 falls straight down), or a **Point source** at **Source x**, **Source y**, **Source z**. **Ray length ℓ** sets how far each reflected ray is drawn, and how far back behind the mirror its virtual extension runs (0 hides the rays). Nothing blocks the light on its way in or out, and it reflects only once. The note says where each caustic is real (ahead of the mirror, where the rays converge) or virtual (behind it), whether it is a surface, a curve, a point, or at infinity, and counts unlit, singular, at-source and stigmatic samples. **Mirror**, **Parameter curves**, **Incident rays** (with the source), **Reflected rays**, **Caustic 1 · μ₁** (rust), **Caustic 2 · μ₂** (slate), and **Virtual rays & caustics** are separate layers; a virtual extension is drawn only for a ray with a caustic point behind the mirror. The shape, light direction or source, ray length, domain and grid are animation tracks, and a reveal grows the mirror along u. Try **A paraboloid gathering light**, **A tilted beam folding into coma**, **A spherical bowl's cusped caustic**, **An ellipsoid refocusing its lamp**, or **A cup's nephroid**, then animate the elevation or move the lamp.

Set **Interaction** to **Refraction · an interface** to make the surface a boundary between two media instead: **Index n₁** on the side n points to, **Index n₂** beyond (each positive, at most 100). **Incident side** replaces **Mirror side**: light arriving from behind is unlit. Each lit sample refracts once by Snell's law, and the transmitted light never meets the surface again. Light leaving the denser side beyond its critical angle is totally reflected: the note counts those samples, and their rays are drawn reflected, in grey, with the **Transmitted rays**. The caustics follow the transmitted rays, real ahead of the interface or virtual behind it.

**Receiver** adds a plane, **Plane x = c**, **y = c** or **z = c**, that collects the outgoing rays. **Plane at c** places it, **Window size s** sets the side of its square window, **Centre** gives the window's centre in the plane's own coordinates, and **Bins** (8–240) divides it. Its shade is irradiance on a logarithmic scale over three decades up to its peak, averaged over each bin: parallel light counts as unit irradiance across its beam, a point source as unit intensity. Nothing is absorbed, there are no Fresnel losses, and nothing casts a shadow. The note says what share of the light the surface intercepts lands in the window, and where the rest went: outside it, never across the plane, totally reflected, or at the edge of the light. **Receiver irradiance** is its own layer, and it appears in a reveal only once every ray is shown. The indices, the plane's position and the window's size are animation tracks. Try **A glass ellipsoid focusing a beam**, **A glass dome's ring of light**, **A lamp in water over air**, or **A cup's nephroid on its floor**.

Choose **Spatial definition · Implicit surface · F(x, y, z) = c** for a level surface: the points where an expression **F(x, y, z)** in x, y, z and **Shape parameter a** (not t) equals **Level c**. It is sought in the box **x from**/**x to**, **y from**/**y to**, **z from**/**z to**, and cut open where it meets the box. **Section planes** (0–24) adds parallel planes with the normal **Normal x**, **Normal y**, **Normal z**, evenly spaced from **First offset d₀** to **Last offset d₁** along it; each cuts the surface in the planar level set of F on it, the kind of curve the 2D notebook draws. The note says how many pieces the mesh has, whether each is closed and of what genus (from its Euler characteristic), where the box cuts it, and counts grid points where F is undefined, sign changes across poles or jumps (marked with crosses and never meshed), vertices without a normal, and grid faces whose corners alternate, where a finer grid is the check. **Level surface**, **Section curves**, **Section planes** and **Box, cuts & open edges** are separate layers. **Sampling & definition** sets **Cells** (4–128 along the box's longest side, at most 262,144 in all) and **Refinement levels** (0–3). Refinement halves the tetrahedra around any edge whose midpoint lies across the level from both its ends, testing the halves in turn, within 100,000 tetrahedra. The note then says how many were bisected, how deep refinement went, and what it left unresolved or whether its budget ran out. The level, a, the section offsets, the cells and the number of planes are animation tracks, and a reveal rises through the box; refinement is not a track. Try:

- **A sphere and its latitudes**
- **The spiric sections of Perseus**
- **Villarceau circles**
- **Two drops meeting** (animate c from 0.8 to 1.3)
- **A double torus**
- **The tanglecube** (animate c down to −14)
- **A gyroid, cut open**
- **A thread between two drops** (set **Refinement levels** to 0 to see what the grid alone misses)

Every curve study, whatever its construction, has **Probe the curve**. Check **Frame, curvature & torsion at a point** and move the **Point** slider (or its arrow, Home and End keys) along the curve's samples: the drawing marks the point with its Frenet frame (T red, N green, B blue), its osculating circle and its own construction lines (for a tangent ruling, involute strings, tangent projections, an inversion's correspondence, a framed ribbon's cross-line, a ruling, or a pursuit's polygon), all in their own colors, while the panel reads out t, **Curvature κ**, **Radius 1/κ** and **Torsion τ** to four significant figures and plots κ and τ along the whole curve, with the point marked. The probe describes the curve itself, not a surface built on it. Where the curve is flat, N, B, τ and the circle are undefined and not drawn, and a straight curve, such as the axis of **Beads that lose their envelope**, says so; where τ cannot be found reliably it says so; a circle too large to draw keeps its radius. Canal, harmonic and vector-field studies show the frame and circle without a construction highlight. Implicit studies have no probe, and a mirror or interface study probes its light or its surface (below), not a curve. More samples give finer steps. While the probe is on, **Animate** offers **Move the probe along the curve**: playback and MP4/WebP export step the probe from the first sample (at progress 0) to the last (at 1) over the unchanged study, and the panel's readout and plot markers follow it, with the **Point** slider locked. Pause and scrub reach any sample, every camera mode applies, and **Stop** returns the probe to your own point. Turning the probe off falls back to revealing and ends a probe animation. With **Vary parameters**, the probe stays on and describes each frame, and **Probe** under the tracks chooses where it stands: **Stays at its t** (the default; absent, with a note in the panel, from a frame whose domain leaves its t out), **Keeps its share of the length** (the same fraction of the drawn curve's length on every frame), or **Moves along the curve** (from the first sample to the last as the tracks play, which cannot loop). A surface's probe **Stays at its row and column** or **Moves along** the surface. Turning the probe off ends such an animation too. Other animations leave the probe out and bring it back when they stop; still images include it and record where it stood.

A surface patch can be probed too, and so can a surface built on a curve: a canal, tangent developable, ruled or framed study (while its ribbon has a width) offers **Describe** (the curve or the surface), and a surface study probes its surface. Check **Principal curvatures & centres at a point**, then move **Along u** and **Along v** on a patch, or **Along t** and **Around** (the turn θ − θ₀ around each contact circle) on a canal, or **Along t** and **Across the ruling**, **Across the ribbon** or **Along the ruling** (the place u on the straight line through the point) on a developable, a framed ribbon or a ruled surface. The drawing shows the point, its normal line, its two principal directions, and each one's normal-section circle and centre (the focal point x + n/κ), in the focal sheets' rust and slate. A canal also draws the contact circle through the point, and the others the ruling. The panel reads out both principal curvatures, their radii 1/κ, the Gaussian curvature K and mean curvature H to four significant figures, and plots both curvatures along the row through the point. A patch numbers them κ₁ ≥ κ₂ as its focal sheets do, and so do a framed ribbon and a ruled surface. A canal names them **κ around the circle** (always −1/R, centred on the curve) and **κ across it**. A developable names them **κ along the ruling** (always 0, its centre at infinity) and **κ across it** (τ/(κ|u|), from the curve's curvature and torsion), so its K is 0 everywhere. A ribbon or ruled surface is never positively curved, K ≤ 0. Curvatures use A = −dn, so a sphere with its outward normal has κ = −1/R. The panel says where the surface has no point (a canal's gap), where it is singular (a collapsed circle, a cuspidal edge, a ruling where the threads meet, or a developable's ruling where the curve straightens), and where the point is an umbilic. A centre beyond 100 study radii is at infinity: its curvature is still read out, but its circle is not drawn. The surface probe snaps to the patch's own grid, or to a canal's mesh rings (at most 481 along t) and 24 turns around each circle. The other surfaces built on a curve use the same rows, with 24 points along a developable's ruling at u = ±L·k/12 (leaving out the edge of regression u = 0, where it has no normal) and 25 across a framed ribbon or along a ruling. With it on, **Animate** offers **Move the probe along the surface** (or **canal surface**, **tangent ribbon**, **framed ribbon**, **ruled surface**), which steps along u or t in the probe's column, as the curve probe's animation does.

A mirror or interface study offers **Describe → The light** (the default) or **The mirror** (**The interface**). The mirror is probed as a surface patch is. The light is probed with **Wavefront, foci & rays at a point**, moving **Along u** and **Along v** over the surface's own grid. The drawing shows the incident ray (from the source, or ℓ back for parallel light), the outgoing ray through both foci, and the outgoing wavefront's two principal directions and normal-section circles, each centred on its focus, which lies on the caustic of the same number, in the caustics' rust and slate. The panel reads out **μ₁** and **μ₂**, the wavefront's principal curvatures (μ₁ ≥ μ₂). It also gives **Foci 1/μ along the ray**, the signed distances to the foci, positive for a real focus ahead of the surface and negative for a virtual one behind it, the **Astigmatic interval** between the foci, and the angle of incidence θ, with the angle of transmission θ′ for an interface. A stigmatic point, where both foci coincide, draws no direction. Unlit points, points beyond the critical angle (drawn with their totally reflected ray), the source and chart singularities have no wavefront, and the panel says which. With it on, **Animate** offers **Move the probe along the mirror** (or **interface**).

A probe plot whose values do not change, to within rounding, is captioned "constant at" that value rather than with the padded range it is drawn in. For example, along u on a surface of revolution lit along its axis, where u turns around the axis, nothing changes.

Every 3D study has **Cut away**. Check **Cut with a plane** to hide everything on the side of a plane that its normal points to, where n̂·p > d. **Normal x**, **Normal y** and **Normal z** give its direction: any nonzero vector, each component within ±100000, and only its direction counts. **Offset d** is the plane's signed distance from the origin along that direction, within ±100000. All four accept constants, as other fields do. A zero normal is refused by name, and nothing is cut until it is fixed. **What it cuts** is **The surface** (the study's own sheet), **Every sheet** (adding offset, focal or caustic sheets and the receiver), or **Sheets and lines** (adding the base curve and every construction line). The probe is never cut. **Face the view** sets the normal toward you from the drawing's current camera, to three decimals, and **Through the center** passes the plane through the middle of the study. **Flip** negates the normal and offset, keeping the plane and hiding the other side. Both wait for any field still being evaluated. **Draw the cut edge** draws where the shown sheets meet the plane, found on their triangles, in graphite or chalk, and the legend names it. The plane stays fixed in space as the camera turns and as parameters animate. The study itself never changes: the cut is applied to the drawing, still images and line drawings, which record it, and animation exports. Choosing an example sets its own cut, or turns the cut off. With the cut on, **Animate** offers **Peel away with the cut**, which moves the offset from the farthest point the cut reaches along the normal, where nothing is hidden, to the nearest, where everything it cuts is hidden. Its fields wait while it plays. Pause and scrub reach any offset, every camera mode applies, and **Stop** returns your plane. Turning the cut off, or leaving its normal zero, falls back to revealing and ends a peel.

Every 3D study has **Lines → Weight**. **Regular**, the default, draws lines as antialiased strokes whose width is a share of the drawing, like the 2D notebook's: the curve heaviest, families of filaments, strands and trajectories lighter, construction lines and receding strings lightest. **Fine** draws them at 0.6 times that and **Bold** at 1.6 times. Because the width scales with the drawing, a still or video at 2000 × 1520 draws lines twice as wide in pixels as a 1000 × 760 one, keeping the picture's proportions. **Hairline** draws every line one device pixel wide at any size, as 3D drawings were before weights, so lines grow fainter as an export grows larger; links made before weights open with it. The weight applies in the drawing, stills, animations and their exports, and the line drawings (SVG) give each path its stroke width. A device whose graphics cannot draw instanced geometry draws hairlines and says so. Choosing an example sets its own weight, or regular.

Every 3D study also has **See through**. **Sheets** is **Opaque**, as before, or **See-through**, which draws every layer of every shown sheet at once: each point of the page shows the mean of the shaded colors of the n sheet layers there, over the background with opacity 1 − (1 − α)ⁿ, so folds, inner sheets and lines inside a surface show, and the drawing is denser where more layers overlap. **Opacity α** is each layer's, from 0.05 to 0.8, and accepts constants; outside that range it is refused by name and the sheets stay opaque until it is fixed. Every layer counts the same, whatever its depth, so intersecting sheets need no sorting; it shows folds and is not a model of light through glass. **Lines behind sheets** is **Hidden** (as before), **Faint** (35% opacity) or **Dashed** (80% opacity, one dash every 1% of the drawing's shorter side, measured along the line in space, so a line receding from view has shorter dashes). A line counts as behind when the nearest sheet is in front of it; a line lying on a sheet is in front, and lines never hide lines. Both apply with opaque or see-through sheets, in the drawing, still images, animations and their exports. The visible-only line drawing draws hidden lines the same way, in groups named `hidden-` and the layer; the every-line drawing is unchanged. If the device's graphics cannot render to half-float targets, see-through sheets are drawn opaque, the panel says so, and exports record it. Choosing an example sets its own, or the plain opaque drawing.

Animation supports progressive reveal, parameter tracks, a camera orbit, a flight through key views, with the probe on the probe moving along the curve or surface (alone, or while parameters vary), and with the cut on a peel, with pause/scrub, the four camera modes, and MP4/WebP export. Every mode but the orbit can also take **Animation camera → Fly through key views**, flying the same views while the geometry moves; each frame is drawn with the camera of its own time, about the study as drawn when playback starts. **Fly through key views** moves only the camera, through 2–12 views taken from the drawing with **+ Add the drawing's view**; **Show** puts the drawing at a view to adjust it, **Set to drawing** takes it back, and **Remove** drops it (the next view's turns are cleared if it becomes the first). Each view has an optional name, shown beside the timeline, and every view after the first has **Turns**, a whole number from −8 to 8 added to the shorter way round (positive as the orbit animation turns; an exact half turn goes the negative way). Views are reached at equal shares of the duration, exactly; **Steady** changes yaw, pitch and zoom (by equal factors) at constant rates on each leg, **Smooth** blends the rates across views without overshooting any. The camera turns about the point in the middle of the page on the plane through the study's center facing it, and while zooming moves that point with the inverse of the zoom. The four camera modes do not apply. Choosing an example sets its own path, or clears the path. While light is traced, **Animation camera → Ride a ray · perspective** follows one representative ray in perspective: choose it with **Ray at u** and **Ray at v** where parameter curves cross, and set **Follow distance** and **Turn window** as optical path in radii of the framing sphere (more than 0, at most 4). The camera looks along the incident ray, turns smoothly over the window centered on its own arrival at the surface, and then looks along the reflected, refracted or totally reflected ray, resting once the ray's head reaches its drawn end. Dragging, panning or zooming a finished ride returns to the held view; Home returns to the ray. **Front**, **Side**, **Top** and **Isometric**, under the 3D and 4D drawings, turn the camera to a named view, keeping zoom and pan; the axis indicator beside them follows the camera. **Projection**, above the drawing, draws the manual camera orthographically (the default), through a perspective lens of 30°, 50° or 90°, or at a chosen angle, a whole number of degrees from 1° to 150° set with **Lens angle** after the named views (starting from the lens shown before, or from the angle last chosen): the plane through the middle of the page keeps its size, zoom moves the eye toward it, and Reset view keeps the projection; animations, exports and links use it. Changing the angle keeps that plane's size and slides the eye, a dolly zoom. Try **A spiral stair, down its well**, **Diving down the stairwell**, **Inside a trefoil's tube** and **Down a torus's tunnel**. **Export image** saves PNG, an SVG with an embedded shaded image, or a line drawing: true SVG paths of the shown lines, either every line or only the lines not hidden behind a surface (sampled at the page's resolution, not exact hidden-line removal), with hidden ones faint or dashed when **Lines behind sheets** says so. The [spatial guide](spatial-study.md) has examples and numerical limits.
