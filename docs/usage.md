# Using Tangent Garden

[Back to the project overview](../README.md)

## Explore

- Thirty-four presets cover representative notebook studies, parallel illumination, pedal-family projections, a normal offset, an offset stack, the generating circles of an envelope, a rolling-circle roulette, a circle rolling on a curve, two curves rolling on curves, two families of circle chords, a family of folding creases, three families of moving circles, three inversions, two Lissajous figures, a Fourier curve of rotating circles, two cyclic pursuits, and two vector fields.
- Input parametric `x(t), y(t)`, Cartesian `y=f(x)`, or polar `r(t)` curves, or generate a roulette, a Lissajous figure, a Fourier curve, a cyclic pursuit, or the trajectories of a vector field. Interval bounds and every numeric parameter field (shape parameter `a`, roulette and rolling-circle radii, tracing distances, and phases, Lissajous amplitudes, frequencies, and phase, Fourier term frequencies, radii, and phases, pursuers' starting coordinates and speeds and the capture distance, vector-field seeds and escape radius, a rolling curve's domain, start, and tracing point, pole and source coordinates, inversion center and radius, travel direction, refractive indices, offset and involute distances) accept constant expressions such as `2*pi`, `-phi`, `pi/2`, or `ln(e)` through the same Go parser. The field keeps the text you entered; an invalid entry is reported with the field's name. Whole-number counts (samples, construction lines, number of offsets) take plain numbers.
- Expressions support `+ - * / ^`, parentheses, `pi ≈ 3.1415926536`, `e ≈ 2.7182818285`, `phi ≈ 1.6180339887` (the positive golden ratio), and the functions shown in the UI. Multiplication is explicit. Exponentiation is right associative; `-t^2` means `-(t^2)`. This is a bounded parser, never JavaScript evaluation. An adjustable `a` is available in curve expressions; A vector field's expressions use `x`, `y`, and `t`. Bounds and animation endpoints must be constant, with no `t`, `x`, `y`, or `a`.
- **Definition → Roulette · rolling circle** traces a point on a circle that rolls without slipping. **Rolling** chooses **Inside a fixed circle** (a hypotrochoid), **Outside a fixed circle** (an epitrochoid), or **Along a line** (a trochoid). Set **Fixed radius R** (not used on a line), **Rolling radius r**, **Tracing distance d** from the rolling center, and **Phase φ** in radians, which turns the tracing arm counterclockwise from the contact point at t = 0. A circle rolling inside must be smaller than the fixed one. Here t is the angle of the rolling center around the fixed center, or the rolling circle's own turn on a line. d = r traces the rim with cusps; larger d gives loops. When R/r is a ratio of whole numbers p/q with q ≤ 200, a note reports that the trace closes after q turns with p arches, and **Trace one full period** sets the domain end to the start plus 2πq; other ratios are never forced closed. The fixed circle or line, the rolling circle at the end of the drawn trace, its center, arm, and contact point are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**, and framing always includes the fixed circle and every rolling position. Any construction tab applies to the roulette. Switching back to an expression format keeps your expressions. **Hypotrochoid & its evolute** is R = 5, r = 2, d = 3 over t from 0 to 4π, whose evolute is a five-pointed star.
- **Definition → Lissajous · A sin(mt + φ), B sin(nt)** traces x = A sin(mt + φ), y = B sin(nt). Set **Amplitude A** and **Amplitude B** (0–100,000), **Frequency m** and **Frequency n** (real, within ±1,000), and **Phase φ** in radians. Each coordinate is drawn as the projection of a point turning on a guide circle: the x guide above the figure and the y guide to its right, with their radii and dashed projection lines to the traced point. **Definition → Fourier · rotating circles** adds 1–16 rotating vectors, each a **Term** with **Frequency k**, **Radius r**, and **Phase φ** (its angle from +x at t = 0, in radians); positive frequencies turn counterclockwise. The vectors are chained from the origin in the order listed, each circle centered where the previous vector ends. **Add a term** appends one turning the other way at the next frequency and half the last radius; **Remove** deletes a term, keeping at least one. For both, t is time, and a note reports closure. When every frequency that moves the curve is a whole number, the curve repeats after t spans 2π divided by their greatest common divisor. Other whole-number ratios, with denominators up to 1,000, close after their common period. Other ratios are never forced closed. **Trace one full period** sets the domain end to the start plus the period, as a multiple of π where it is one. Terms of zero radius or amplitude do not affect closure. The guides, circles, vectors, and traced point are drawn where the trace ends and hide with **Construction lines**; framing includes every circle at every representative sample. Any construction tab applies, and switching back to an expression format keeps your expressions. **Lissajous 3 : 2 & its pedal** is x = sin(3t + π/2), y = sin 2t with its four-petalled pedal about the center. **Epicycles, turned inside out** is e^{it} + 0.45e^{−4it} + 0.2e^{6it}, a five-armed star, inverted in the unit circle into a flower. **Lissajous √2 : 1, never closing** fills its box without repeating, and so does its pedal.
- **Definition → Pursuit · each chases the next** sets 2–16 pursuers, each with **Start x**, **Start y** (its position when t is at the domain start, within ±100,000) and **Speed v** (0–100,000). Each runs straight at the next, the last at the first; t is time. **Add a pursuer** appends one halfway from the last back to the first at the last one's speed; **Remove** keeps at least two; **Space evenly on a circle** places them counterclockwise, in chase order, on the circle about their centroid through the farthest, starting from the first, keeping speeds. A pursuer's direction is undefined on its target, so the chase stops, for everyone, the first time any pursuer comes within **Capture distance ε** of its own target; later samples are left empty, and nobody merges or changes target. A note names the pair and time, and **End the domain at the capture** sets the domain end there. Every pursuer's path is drawn as a curve; the polygons connecting their positions at each representative sample and the pursuers at the end of the drawn chase are construction geometry and hide with **Construction lines**. Framing includes every path. Constructions apply to the first pursuer's path. **Seven pursuers & an evolute** starts from the unit heptagon at unit speed: the polygons stay regular as they turn and shrink, every path is a logarithmic spiral, and the evolute of the first is the same spiral scaled by tan(π/7). **Four chasers at unequal speeds** starts from a square with speeds 1, 1.25, 1, and 0.8; the square skews as it turns, and the third catches the slow fourth at t ≈ 1.96 while the others are still apart.
- **Definition → Vector field · trajectories** follows the field whose velocity at (x, y) at time t is (**dx/dt**, **dy/dt**). These are expressions in `x`, `y`, `t`, and `a`.
  - **Seeds:** set 1–16 seeds, each with **Seed x** and **Seed y** (its position when t is at the domain start, within ±100,000). **Add a seed** continues the line through the last two seeds by one more step, or puts the new seed half a unit to the right of a single one. **Remove** keeps at least one. Each trajectory follows the field on its own, and t is time.
  - **Ends:** a trajectory ends where it leaves the circle of **Escape radius R** about the origin (0–100,000), where the field stops being finite or changes too fast to follow, or where its integration step budget runs out. A seed outside the circle has no path. A note says where and why each trajectory that ended early did so.
  - **Drawing:** every trajectory is drawn as a curve, and constructions apply to the first. The escape circle (dashed), the seeds (rings), chevrons showing each trajectory's direction of travel at each representative sample, and the direction field are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**. The direction field is a grid of arrows spanning the trajectories. It is drawn only when neither expression mentions t, because a field that changes with t has no single direction field. Framing includes every trajectory, but not the escape circle or the grid.
  - **Van der Pol limit cycle** is ẋ = y, ẏ = a(1 − x²)y − x with a = 1. A trajectory from near the origin and one from far out wind onto the same closed orbit, with a stack of parallel curves winding with the first.
  - **Pendulum phase portrait** is ẋ = y, ẏ = −sin x. Three swings stay on closed orbits, with the evolute of the first. Four seeds above the separatrix go over the top and leave the escape circle, mirrored pairs at equal times.
- Point sources default to Cartesian x/y independently of curve format. Choose **Source coordinates → Polar** for nonnegative radius and theta in radians, counterclockwise from +x. Switching coordinates preserves the location. For parallel light, 0° travels right and 90° travels up.
- Refraction uses **incident index n₁ / transmitted index n₂**. Normals face each incident ray. Total internal reflection produces an amber reflected ray, with no transmitted envelope at that sample.
- **Ellipse & its pedal** projects an independent pole onto the tangent at each sampled point. Set **Pole x** and **Pole y** in Cartesian coordinates; the pole may lie on the curve. Each representative construction has a tangent segment and a perpendicular from the pole to its foot. Both follow the Construction lines toggle; the count selects representative constructions, each with two segments. A gold marker identifies the pole; these settings do not change the optical source.
- The **Pedal** tab holds the whole pole family. Its **Projection** selector chooses **Pedal** (foot on the tangent), **Contrapedal** (foot on the normal), or **Orthotomic** (the pole reflected across the tangent). Switching keeps the pole, and leaving and returning to the tab keeps the selection. **Ellipse & its contrapedal** projects the center onto each normal. **Ellipse & its orthotomic** reflects a focus across each tangent; the reflections land on a circle about the other focus. Orthotomic constructions draw the genuine foot like a pedal, then dash the reflected half from the foot to the orthotomic point.
- The **Offset** tab moves every point a signed **Offset distance d** along its normal. Positive distances go to the left of the direction of travel, inward on a counterclockwise closed curve; negative distances go to the right. Each construction line is a normal segment of length |d|. Once d exceeds a radius of curvature, the offset folds into cusps on the evolute and swallowtail loops; these are part of the curve, not errors. **Flower & its offset** moves 0.4 inward from a five-petal polar curve whose tips curve more tightly than that. The offset distance is independent of the involute's string offset. **Offsets → A stack of offsets** instead draws **Number of offsets** (2–64) parallel curves evenly spaced from **First offset distance** to **Last offset distance**, both included; each construction line crosses the whole stack along a normal and reaches back to the curve. Offsets × samples may not exceed 131,072. **Generating circles** draws a circle at each construction line with the largest distance as its radius, |d| for one offset; the offsets at ±radius are their envelope. The circles are construction geometry: they hide with **Construction lines**, but framing always keeps them in view. **Flower & its offset stack** reproduces eighteen offsets 0.105 apart; **Circles & their envelope** rolls a radius-0.5 coin around an ellipse. The single distance and the stack settings are remembered separately when switching.
- The **Rolling** tab rolls a circle without slipping along the curve itself, whatever its definition, and traces a point fixed to it. **Side of the curve** puts the circle on the **Left of travel** (inside a counterclockwise closed curve) or the **Right of travel**. Set **Circle radius ρ**, **Tracing distance ℓ** from the circle's center, and **Phase ψ** in radians, which turns the tracing arm counterclockwise from the contact at the domain start. ℓ = 0 traces a parallel curve, ℓ = ρ the rim with cusps on the curve, and larger ℓ loops. Each construction line joins a contact point to its tracing point and is normal to the traced curve. The circle, its center, arm, contact, and tracing point are drawn where the trace ends and hide with **Construction lines**. The circle starts rolling at the domain start and stops, with a numerical note, at an invalid sample or a cusp; it may overlap the curve where it is larger than the radius of curvature, as the mathematical roulette does. On a circle or line it matches the **Roulette** definition. **Flower & a rolling circle** rolls a rim point inside the five-petal flower, with ten circumferences nearly one lap, so its ten cusps touch the petal tips and waists.
- **Rolling shape → Curve · x(t), y(t)** rolls a second curve instead of a circle. Enter **Rolling x(t)** and **Rolling y(t)** in the rolling curve's own frame (they may use `a`), its domain **Rolling t from**/**to**, the point that first touches the base, **Contact starts at t**, and a **Tracing point** (x, y) in the same frame. Equal arc lengths are rolled on both curves, which stay tangent at the contact. On the **Left of travel** the contact runs toward the end of the rolling curve's domain and its own left side faces the base's left; on the **Right** it runs toward the start and its left side faces the base's right, so a counterclockwise closed curve rolls inside or outside as a circle does. A closed rolling curve wraps around indefinitely; an open one, or one with a cusp or undefined point, stops there with a numerical note. The rolling curve is drawn where the trace ends, with the contact, tracing point, and the line joining them, and hides with **Construction lines**. The circle's settings are kept while the curve is selected. **Ellipse rolling on an ellipse** rolls an ellipse around its twin from matching vertices: its focus traces a circle of radius 2a about the far focus. **Ellipse rolling in a flower** rolls an ellipse whose perimeter is a fifth of the flower's inside it, so its vertex returns to each petal tip and the trace closes a five-pointed star.
- The **Envelope** tab draws a family of lines or circles, one for each point of the curve, and the curve they all touch. **Family → Chords to a second point** joins the curve's point at t to **Second point x(t)**, **y(t)** at the same t (they may use `a`); each chord is drawn between its endpoints, and the second points are drawn as a thin curve with the base. Envelope points on a chord's extension, beyond the segment, are dashed and hide with **Virtual extensions**; **Extend chords to full lines** draws the full lines instead, with no virtual part. Where the two endpoints coincide the chord has no direction: it is omitted and the envelope has a gap, with a numerical note. **Family → Lines turned to an angle θ(t)** instead turns each line through the curve's point to **Direction angle θ(t)**, in radians counterclockwise from +x; these lines are unbounded and drawn across the whole view. A direction turning by exactly π is the same line. Where neighbouring lines are parallel they meet only at infinity and the envelope has a gap. **Chords & a cardioid** joins angle t to angle 2t on the unit circle with x = cos(a·t), y = sin(a·t) and a = 2; its chords envelope a cardioid. **Chords of four** is the 200-chord reference study, t to 4t, whose envelope has three cusps. **Folding a parabola** creases a sheet so the point (0, 1) lands on each point of the x-axis; the creases, at angle atan(x), envelope the parabola x² = 4y. **Family → Circles of radius R(t)** centers a circle of **Circle radius R(t)** (it may use `a`) on each point of the curve, drawn with the construction lines along with its radii to the points where it touches the envelope. The envelope has two branches, to the left and right of travel, drawn in the derived colour. They merge where the radius changes exactly as fast as the center moves (|R′| equals the curve's speed), and vanish where it changes faster: the circles nest, and a note counts the gaps. A branch that collapses to one point is drawn as a dot. Where the radius is not positive there is no circle, and a stationary center has no envelope point; both are counted in notes. A constant radius gives the offsets ±R. **Circles through a focus** centers circles on a parabola so that each passes through its focus: they touch the directrix, and their other branch is the focus itself. **Breathing circles** swells and shrinks circles around a circle with radius 1 + 0.35 sin(a·t). **Swelling circles** grows its radius faster than its center moves in places, so its branches break into pieces.
- The **Inversion** tab inverts a curve in a circle: each point p moves along its ray from the **Inversion center** (x, y) to the point whose distance from the center, times p's, is the square of **Inversion radius R**. **Invert** chooses **The curve itself**, whatever its definition (roulettes included), or one of its derived curves: **Its evolute**, **Its pedal**, **Its contrapedal**, **Its orthotomic** (with **Pole x**, **Pole y**, shared with the Pedal tab), or **Its offset** (with **Offset distance d**, shared with the Offset tab). A derived curve is drawn faintly with the base curve. The image is the derived curve. The dashed circle of inversion and the segments joining points to their images, along rays from the center, are construction lines; the center is marked. Where the inverted curve passes through the center, its image runs off to infinity and is left open there, never joined across, with a numerical note; a pass close to the center between samples is found too, and more samples resolve it. A point exactly on the center has no image. Where the curve runs off to infinity, its image passes through the center. **Hyperbola into a lemniscate** inverts x² − y² = 1 about its center into Bernoulli's lemniscate. **Hypotrochoid, turned inside out** inverts a roulette, so the loops nearest the center become the outer petals. **An ellipse's pedal, inverted** inverts the pedal about its pole into the ellipse's polar reciprocal, another ellipse.
- The involute is `r − (s+c)T`, with `s=0` at the interval's lower bound and adjustable initial offset `c`.
- Toggle construction lines, incident rays, dashed backward extensions, curves, and axes. Drag to pan, scroll to zoom, or fit the view. Light/dark backgrounds and image export preserve the visible drawing. **Export image** in the header opens a menu: **PNG image** (2000 × 1520, drawn from the vectors at that size, so it is sharp and works almost anywhere) or **SVG** (vector, scalable; its metadata includes the mathematical inputs). Both capture exactly what is shown, including pan/zoom and a paused animation frame.
- Simple mode offers the original slider and sample presets. Expert mode accepts any whole number of samples from 64–32,768 and construction lines from 2–2,048, with no more lines than samples. Switching modes preserves custom values. These workload limits apply in both modes and to animations. They are application guardrails, not intrinsic browser limits: computation and serialization grow with sample count, while SVG drawing grows with line count. Responsiveness depends on hardware, expressions, and construction. More samples improve spatial coverage but do not automatically improve derivative precision.
- The theme initially follows your system, including live time-of-day changes. Clicking the toggle saves an explicit light/dark preference in this browser. **Follow system** clears that override. Storage failures do not prevent using the app.
- On desktop, the parameter sidebar scrolls independently of the drawing. On narrow screens, the drawing and controls stack in the document.
- The expandable diacaustic explanation covers index conventions, Snell's law, total internal reflection, and the supported inclusive range 0.01–10. Every finite decimal in that range is allowed; there is no 0.05-step restriction.

## Animate

Open **Animation** in the sidebar. Set a duration (0.1–3,600 seconds) and choose one of two modes:

**Draw along the curve** reveals the study from the domain minimum to maximum. It reveals the already-computed base curve, derived curve, and representative lines in parameter order. The numerical grid and arc-length anchor stay fixed; this avoids changing the involute as the drawing grows. At the start, a zero-length trace is represented by its first point, not by an invalid zero-width computation domain.

**Vary parameters** adds one or more simultaneous linear tracks with **From** and **To** values. Every numeric simulation control is available: domain start/end, point-source x/y or polar radius/theta, pedal-family pole x/y, inversion center x/y and radius (with the inverted curve's pole or offset distance), offset distance or the first/last distance and number of offsets in a stack, roulette radii, tracing distance, and phase, Lissajous phase, frequencies, and amplitudes, each Fourier term's phase, radius, and frequency, each pursuer's speed and starting x and y and the capture distance, each vector-field seed's x and y and the escape radius, rolling-circle radius, tracing distance, and phase, rolling-curve tracing point and contact start, the chord multiplier and the circles' lobes through `a`, parallel travel direction, incident/transmitted index, involute offset, sample count, line count, and shape parameter `a` (not for roulettes, harmonic curves, or pursuits, which do not use it; a vector field keeps it for its expressions). Ray length is also animatable. Only parameters applicable to the selected construction/source are offered. Counts are rounded to whole numbers; invalid endpoints are rejected with their start/end context, and a calculation error stops playback. Moving the domain start to its end creates a zero-width interval; derivative-based constructions need a positive width of at least 0.000001. Singular samples remain numerical gaps rather than stopping a valid study.

Examples:

- **Ellipse & its pedal** → Vary parameters → Pole x from `1.65` to `-1.65`. Add Pole y for simultaneous motion. Stop restores the original pole; scrubbing and image/animation exports use the same computed projections.
- **Flower & its offset** → Vary parameters → Offset distance d from `-0.4` to `0.8`. Outward, swallowtails form at the concave waists once |d| passes about 0.183, their least radius of curvature; inward, they form at the petal tips once d passes about 0.245. Near d = 0 the offset is smooth.
- **Flower & its offset stack** → Vary parameters → Number of offsets from `2` to `18`, or Last offset distance from `0` to `0.84`, grows the stack a member at a time. **Draw along the curve** reveals every member and circle together along the base curve.
- **Seven pursuers & an evolute** → Draw along the curve runs the chase in time, adding each connecting polygon as it is reached. Vary parameters → Speed v₂ from `1` to `1/phi` slows the second pursuer: the first closes on it long before the heptagon would collapse, and the chase stops there.
- **Van der Pol limit cycle** → Draw along the curve runs both trajectories together in time, so the one from far out and the one from near the origin are shown at the same moment. Vary parameters → Shape parameter a from `1` to `4` sharpens the cycle into slow drifts and fast jumps.
- **Epicycles, turned inside out** → Draw along the curve turns the chained vectors as the star is traced, and its inverse grows with it. Vary parameters → Phase φ₂ from `0` to `2*pi` turns the second vector's starting angle through a full turn, twisting the star and its petals and returning them to the start; Radius r₂ from `0.45` to `0.1` draws the arms in, and the petals shrink toward the circle of inversion.
- **Hypotrochoid & its evolute** → Draw along the curve rolls the circle around the fixed one as the trace grows; more construction lines give smoother motion. Vary parameters → Phase φ from `0` to `2*pi` turns the arm through a full revolution, and Tracing distance d from `0` to `3` grows the loops out of a circle, through the rim's cusps at d = 2.
- **Ellipse rolling in a flower** → Draw along the curve rolls the ellipse around the inside of the flower. Vary parameters → Tracing point x from `0.3` to `0` moves the tracing point from the vertex to the ellipse's center, opening the star's cusps into a smooth rosette; Contact starts at t from `0` to `pi/2` starts the roll on the ellipse's flank instead.
- **Flower & a rolling circle** → Draw along the curve rolls the circle around the inside of the flower. Vary parameters → Tracing distance ℓ from `0` to `0.3` grows the trace from a parallel curve through the rim's cusps into loops; Phase ψ from `0` to `2*pi` slides the cusps once around each arch.
- **Chords & a cardioid** → Vary parameters → Shape parameter a from `2` to `10` runs through the multiplication tables: each whole number m draws an epicycloid with m − 1 cusps, and the chords between them rearrange. Draw along the curve adds the chords one phase at a time.
- **Breathing circles** → Vary parameters → Shape parameter a from `5` to `8`: the circles gain lobes, and once |R′| outruns the center's speed, past a = 40/7, the two branches pinch together and break into pieces.
- **Hyperbola into a lemniscate** → Vary parameters → Inversion center x from `0` to `1.5`: as the center approaches the hyperbola's vertex at x = 1, the lemniscate's right loop swells toward infinity; at x = 1 the curve passes through the center and the image is open there; beyond it, the right branch's image closes into a large loop around the rest. Inversion radius R from `2` to `1` shrinks the image fourfold, with R².
- **Unwinding a circle** → Draw along the curve → 10 seconds → Hold final view.
- **Light inside a circle** → Vary parameters → Source x from `1` to `.75` → 30 seconds. Add Source y for a moving source in both coordinates.
- For an orbit: set a polar curve `r(t)=1`, domain `0` to `2*pi`, and a polar point source with radius `1` and theta `0`. Animate **Source theta θ (radians)** from `0` to `pi/2`. The source follows a quarter circle; radius may have a simultaneous track. Polar sources also work with Cartesian and parametric curves.
- **Parallel light & a circle** → Vary parameters → Travel direction from `0` to `360` → 30 seconds. Angles here are degrees, and interpolation follows the entered values without shortest-path wrapping.
- Write `a*cos(t)` and `a*sin(t)` and animate `a` from `1` to `phi`. More generally, expressions such as `(1-a)*cos(t)+a*cos(2*t)` allow shape transitions; categorical choices such as construction type and curve format are held fixed during a run.

Four camera choices are available:

| Camera | Behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Hold final view | Fit the final result once and retain its center and scale throughout. |
| Hold current view | Capture your current manual pan/zoom for playback and export. |
| Follow center, fixed zoom | Use the final scale, but center on the current geometry. Parameter animations that grow larger than the final extent can still leave the frame. |
| Fit each frame | Fit the current geometry on every displayed frame. |

Runs use a clean automatic camera except when **Hold current view** captures your manual framing. **Pause** freezes the current frame; the timeline then scrubs by dragging, clicking, or with the arrow, Home, and End keys, and image export (PNG or SVG) captures that frame; SVG also records its animation metadata. **Resume** continues for the remaining duration; **Replay** restarts. **Stop** restores the original study and manual pan/zoom. After completion, controls unlock and the button becomes **Reset view**; editing settings clears the completed preview for another run. Changing a simulation input also cancels the animation. Playback pauses when the tab becomes hidden.

Playback targets at most 30 displayed frames per second, using elapsed time rather than a fixed number of steps. A longer duration gives smaller parameter changes per frame. Only one live frame calculation is in flight; slower devices skip intermediate times instead of accumulating a backlog. Endpoint preparation is outside the requested playback duration, and the last frame is always the exact endpoint.

**Export format** chooses between **MP4 video** (H.264; small files that play on phones, in messaging and social apps, and in browsers) and **animated WebP** (can loop; plays in browsers). MP4 files are typically far smaller at similar quality. The page offers only the formats this browser can encode, and the picker appears only when both work. Safari and every iOS browser cannot encode WebP, so they offer MP4 alone. MP4 is the default when available. MP4 files have no loop setting, so the loop checkbox applies to WebP only; video players decide whether to loop. If the browser's video encoder refuses the chosen resolution, the export button explains that instead of switching formats silently.

The export button (**Export animated WebP** or **Export MP4 video**) saves the entire configured animation, including both endpoints, with independent **Export resolution** and **Export quality** sliders. Resolution ranges from **500 × 380 to 2000 × 1520** in 250 × 190 increments; quality ranges from **1 to 100**. Defaults are **2000 × 1520**, with quality **60 for MP4** and **85 for WebP**; lossy WebP needs the higher setting to look clean, while MP4 at 60 is hard to tell from 95 at a fraction of the size. Each format keeps its own quality, so switching formats and back preserves your setting. The reset button restores the resolution and the current format's quality default. Resolution controls the actual SVG rasterization dimensions, not an upscale of a finished image. Quality controls compression independently (for MP4 it sets the maximum bitrate; smooth line art often encodes well below it, so MP4 size may change little): lower values make smaller files at the cost of detail. The larger default matters: at 1000 × 760 construction lines are about one pixel wide, and video and lossy WebP store colour at half resolution, so thin lines lose their colour and soften. Choose a smaller size for smaller files and faster export, and raise quality only if you see artifacts; quality 100 remains available. Twice the width and height means four times the pixels, and maximum encoding quality can cause a large additional jump in file size. Size is not proportional to the quality slider; it depends on the drawing and browser. Maximum quality is not a cross-browser lossless guarantee. Choose 15 or 30 fps, or 60 fps for MP4 (animated WebP is limited to 30; choosing WebP after 60 uses 30, and switching back restores 60). The default is 30 fps. A WebP can optionally loop; the default plays once and holds its final frame. The export snapshots the theme and visible layers and uses the selected animation camera, including manual framing for **Hold current view**, independently of the current playback position. Frames are rendered individually with the same SVG renderer and encoded at the selected quality in the browser. Rendering may take longer than playback, but the saved duration is preserved to the nearest millisecond. Frame delays divide that duration evenly (within 1 ms); endpoints are included on the first and last frames.

Export shows progress and can be canceled. Parameter-animation exports calculate several frames at once on multi-core devices (at most two at a time on phones and tablets, to limit memory) and discard those extra calculations on cancel. Editing a simulation input also cancels it without saving a partial file. Limits are 7,200 frames (two minutes at 60 fps, four at 30, or eight at 15) and 256 MiB of compressed output. A browser that can encode neither format disables the export button and says so; single frames can still be saved as SVG. No file is ever mislabeled as a format it is not. Everything stays local, and static hosting remains sufficient.

This is a **mathematical construction explorer**, not a scene renderer. Every regular sampled point participates; there is no occlusion, solid-medium topology, or multiple-bounce tracing. A closed curve can therefore display ray families that would not all be illuminated in a physical object.
